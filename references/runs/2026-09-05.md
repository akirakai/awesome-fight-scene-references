## Martial Arts

### 2025 17th World Wushu Championships — Singapore Men's Duilian

- Category: Martial Arts / Duilian / Paired Choreography
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4PClp18aTjM
- Why it is worth studying: Competitive duilian is unusually useful as clean paired-action data because the attack and response are designed together and the full bodies remain readable.
- Action design: synchronized attacker/responder timing, near-contact weapon/body lines, role reversals, jumps/rolls and rapid recoveries. The choreography demonstrates how two performers can look extremely fast without both moving randomly at the same time.
- Camera / editing: neutral competition framing preserves both performers, spacing and body orientation instead of hiding mechanics behind cuts.
- AI-video takeaways: structure paired combat as explicit roles: A initiates → B evades/parries → B becomes attacker → A redirects → both recover. Keep both bodies visible during the contact phrase before adding cinematic close-ups.
- Tags: duilian, wushu, paired-routine, two-person, timing, full-body-reference, 2025

### The Villainess — Bus Fight

- Category: Film / Moving-Vehicle Close Quarters
- Watch: https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_villainess/videos/c6sr4A2jXoRk
- Alternate clip hub: https://www.martialartsentertainment.com/the-villainess-2017/
- Why it is worth studying: A compact moving-vehicle fight where the environment naturally constrains movement and keeps the protagonist's immediate tactical problem clear.
- Action design: narrow-aisle entries, short strikes and grapples, balance corrections and rapid target handoffs. The bus motion adds instability without replacing the underlying body mechanics.
- Camera / editing: close energetic coverage stays aligned with the vehicle's longitudinal axis, using the aisle and seats as persistent spatial anchors.
- AI-video takeaways: for a 10–15 second vehicle fight, lock one spatial axis and use only one active threat at a time: entry → seat/rail interaction → counter → target switch → finish. Explicitly prompt balance recovery after large vehicle movement.

## Anime

### Shangri-La Frontier — Sunraku Fights His First Boss

- Category: Anime / Human-vs-Creature Boss Fight
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VGWjGNANsjU
- Source: Crunchyroll official clip.
- Why it is worth studying: A clean asymmetric fight against the Ravenous Python, useful for understanding how a smaller agile character should react to a much larger threat instead of sharing the same movement grammar.
- Action design: boss telegraph → evasive reposition → short punish window → separation/reset. The large enemy controls the broad motion while Sunraku uses fast local movement and timing.
- Camera / editing: scale is preserved by alternating readable wider geography with closer impact beats rather than staying permanently tight.
- AI-video takeaways: for human-vs-monster AI action, define the boss's telegraph first, then the hero's response. Do not make both characters attack continuously; one actor should establish the motion problem and the other should solve it.
- Tags: anime, boss-fight, asymmetric-combat, dodge, punish-window, creature-fight, readable-scale

## Prompt patterns distilled from this batch

1. **Paired fight timing:** attacker A commits → defender B responds → role reversal → both recover before the next phrase.
2. **Narrow-space action:** lock one environment axis, one active opponent and one physical interaction per station.
3. **Asymmetric boss fight:** boss telegraph → hero evasive response → short punish window → reset and re-establish scale.
4. **Reference-video workflow:** motion reference owns choreography; character/scene refs own appearance; prompt should not invent conflicting moves.
5. **AI sword-motion evaluation:** body mechanics and weapon orientation first, speed lines/afterimages/VFX second.
