# Fight Scene Reference Batch — 2026-09-15

Seven newly screened references for AI-video action design. This batch was checked against the current category indexes and recent dated batches to avoid title-level duplication. It intentionally mixes clean real-world motion, classic film/anime choreography, recent game footage, stunt previs, and a reference-driven AI video benchmark.

## Martial Arts

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Zhang Yaling Women's Nandao Gold Medal Performance

- Category: Martial Arts / Performance / Nandao
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=15IAYMOtW9s
- Official results: https://www.iwuf.org/xhimg/soft/250923/16-250923212437.pdf
- Result: Zhang Yaling (China) won the women's Nandao final with 9.746 at the 17th World Wushu Championships in Brasília.
- Why it is worth studying: Nandao gives a very clean reference for a heavy single-edged weapon with explosive Southern-style body mechanics. The stable competition framing exposes stance depth, hip/shoulder rotation, weapon orientation, leaps and recovery without cinematic cutting hiding the mechanics.
- Action design: low rooted stance → committed diagonal or horizontal blade path → whole-body turn → level change / jump → hard landing → held recovery. The weapon should remain rigid and consistently aligned with the hands throughout the phrase.
- Camera / editing: neutral full-body competition framing is ideal for extracting 2–5 second motion-reference phrases because the performer rarely leaves frame and there are no motivated cuts to compensate for incomplete action.
- AI-video takeaways: lock foot placement, torso rotation, both-hand relationship and blade axis first. Add hair, costume movement, sparks or a moving camera only after the body/weapon trajectory is stable.
- Tags: wushu, nandao, zhang-yaling, iwuf, weapon-form, long-blade, full-body, motion-reference, 2025

## Film / TV

### The Villainess — Opening First-Person Assault

- Category: Film / TV / POV / One-vs-Many
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O5BtU2LetrU
- Film / trailer context: https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_villainess
- Why it is worth studying: The opening turns a large one-vs-many fight into a sequence of local, readable problems by putting the viewer inside the protagonist's movement. It is a strong reference for aggressive first-person or body-mounted AI camera work without losing target order.
- Action design: forward entry → immediate close-range threat → strike / weapon interaction → target drops or leaves frame → camera-body reorients → next threat enters. The fight advances through target handoffs rather than having the whole crowd attack simultaneously.
- Camera / editing: first-person POV, fast body turns and hidden transitions create apparent continuity. The camera behaves like the fighter's head/upper torso, so every pan is motivated by a new threat instead of arbitrary visual energy.
- AI-video takeaways: for a 10–15 second POV fight, keep one active target at a time and describe the camera as physically attached to the fighter: lunge → contact → recoil → snap-turn → reacquire next attacker. Hide only one transition behind a wall/body/whip turn rather than stacking cuts.
- Tags: the-villainess, pov, one-vs-many, hidden-cut, target-handoff, body-camera, korean-action

### The Night Comes for Us — Ito vs. Arian Final Fight

- Category: Film / TV / Silat / Two-Person Duel
- Watch clip: https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x8guhbl
- Official film: https://www.netflix.com/title/80148162
- Why it is worth studying: Joe Taslim and Iko Uwais sustain a long duel through clear phases instead of one flat stream of combinations. The escalation from empty-hand exchanges into increasingly desperate close-range violence is especially useful for designing a fight arc.
- Action design: technical entry/read → short attack-counter phrase → separation → harder re-entry → environmental or weapon escalation → fatigue / rougher mechanics → decisive finish. Each new phase changes the tactical problem rather than just increasing speed.
- Camera / editing: medium and medium-wide coverage preserves hips, shoulders and contact lines through fast exchanges; cuts generally support a new angle, position or escalation beat instead of replacing the choreography.
- AI-video takeaways: for a 15-second duel, use three phases: clean technical exchange → pressure/fatigue → brutal finishing burst. Preserve small resets and imperfect recovery so the bodies feel physical rather than motion-smoothed.
- Tags: the-night-comes-for-us, iko-uwais, joe-taslim, silat, duel, escalation, fatigue, grounded-action

## Games

### Avatar Legends: The Fighting Game — Gameplay Overview

- Category: Games / 2D Fighting / Elemental Combat
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RsuaeS1pM9g
- Game overview: https://www.gematsu.com/games/avatar-legends-the-fighting-game
- Why it is worth studying: The game is hand-drawn in 2D and built around movement-centric combat, which makes spacing, silhouette and attack state unusually explicit. Different elemental fighters keep strongly distinct visual vocabularies while sharing the same side-on camera grammar.
- Action design: neutral spacing → movement/feint → one committed elemental attack → defender block/evade/counter → support or special-move interruption → return to neutral. The fixed plane makes attack direction and range easy to read.
- Camera / editing: side-on framing stays stable during normal combat, while special attacks provide controlled moments for larger visual emphasis. The consistency is useful when diagnosing whether an AI fight's motion is readable before adding cinematic camera movement.
- AI-video takeaways: prototype choreography with a simple camera first. Assign each fighter one movement vocabulary and one elemental rule, then add only one cinematic reframe for a special move or finisher.
- Tags: avatar-legends, fighting-game, 2d, elemental-combat, spacing, silhouette, movement-vocabulary, 2026

## Anime

### Fate/stay night: Heaven's Feel III — Rider vs. Saber Alter

- Category: Anime / High-Speed Duel / Supernatural Sword Combat
- Watch: https://vimeo.com/563525330
- Official film: https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/GEVUW3ZWK/fatestay-night-heavens-feel-iii-spring-song
- Why it is worth studying: This is a strong example of extreme speed remaining readable because the sequence repeatedly returns to clear silhouettes, attack vectors and impact states. Rider and Saber Alter also maintain very different locomotion and power signatures throughout the exchange.
- Action design: readable stance / line of attack → explosive displacement → clash or near-miss → hard separation → orientation reset → stronger re-entry. Large supernatural effects amplify a contact or state change rather than replacing the underlying body relationship.
- Camera / editing: rapid reframing and large spatial jumps are anchored by strong poses and directional continuity; the viewer repeatedly gets a clean frame to understand who is where before the next speed burst.
- AI-video takeaways: lock only 4–6 anchor states for a 15-second anime fight: guard → burst entry → clash → displaced recovery → second entry → finish. Let smear, light trails and camera travel bridge those anchors instead of prompting every micro-movement.
- Tags: fate, heavens-feel, rider, saber-alter, anime-duel, speed-burst, anchor-pose, supernatural-combat

## Stunt / Choreography

### Sword Concepts — One & Done Stunts Chicago

- Category: Stunt / Choreography / Sword Previs
- Watch: https://vimeo.com/1045232525
- Credits: fight choreography by Greg Poljacik and Alex Hashioka; pre-vis action design by Emmanuel Manzanares.
- Why it is worth studying: This is low-noise material created around sword action itself rather than story, VFX or production design. That makes blade path, partner timing, attack ownership, camera side and recovery poses easy to isolate.
- Action design: measure / guard → committed cut or thrust → parry / redirect → angle change → counter → disengage. Different performers demonstrate variations while preserving complete physical phrases.
- Camera / editing: previs-style framing prioritizes the exchange and keeps enough body visible to understand footwork and weapon geometry, making it useful for direct V2V motion-reference clipping.
- AI-video takeaways: crop 2–5 second phrases and let the source video control body trajectory, blade contact timing and camera side. Use character references for identity and prompts only for environment, lighting and surface style; do not re-invent the choreography.
- Tags: swordfight, stunt-previs, lbp-stunts, one-and-done-stunts, weapon-path, camera-side, motion-reference

## AI Action

### Same Reference Video Benchmark — Seedance 2.5 vs. MiniMax H3 (8-Group Test)

- Category: AI Action / Reference-to-Video / Benchmark
- Watch: https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1V8bQ61ERg/
- Published: 2026-08-16
- Why it is worth studying: The creator uses the same character image, scene image and white-model motion reference across Seedance 2.5, 2.0, Fast, Mini and MiniMax H3 local/API variants, making this unusually useful for isolating model behavior from prompt/reference differences.
- Evaluation focus: character identity consistency, environment reconstruction, body trajectory, reference adherence and temporal jumps. In this creator's test setup, local H3 was judged strongest overall on reference consistency; that result should be treated as a benchmark observation, not a universal model ranking.
- Camera / editing: side-by-side comparison makes motion drift, sudden framing changes and state discontinuities easier to detect than in a polished single-model showcase.
- AI-video takeaways: benchmark fight models with identical inputs and score at least four independent axes: identity, body trajectory/contact timing, scene/camera adherence, and temporal continuity. A model that looks prettier but misses the reference trajectory is not necessarily the better V2V fight model.
- Tags: seedance-2-5, minimax-h3, reference-video, v2v, benchmark, motion-adherence, identity-consistency, 2026
