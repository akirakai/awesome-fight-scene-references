# Fight Reference Batch — 2026-08-23

A curated batch of non-duplicate references selected for choreography clarity, camera value, interaction timing, and usefulness as AI-video reference material.

## Film / TV

### Monkey Man — Kitchen Fight
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_1PuVDh14h4
- Type: Film / close-quarters environmental fight
- Why study it: an official Universal Pictures clip built around a cramped working kitchen and improvised culinary objects, with a scrappy underdog fighting style rather than polished tournament technique.
- Action design: short attack/counter bursts move from one nearby object or surface to the next, so every environmental interaction creates the next tactical problem.
- Camera / editing: close handheld/tracking energy stays attached to the protagonist; tight reframing increases impact without abandoning the kitchen geography.
- AI-video takeaway: excellent 15-second kitchen-fight template. Define 3–4 stations—prep table → hanging utensils → stove/counter → exit—and attach one explicit attack/response beat to each station. Keep the hero identity locked while the camera follows.
- Tags: monkey-man, kitchen-fight, improvised-weapons, environmental-action, handheld, close-quarters

- Source / gameplay trailer: https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/06/06/son-of-thanjai-brings-an-ancient-empire-to-life/
- Type: Game / Kalaripayattu-inspired weapon combat
- Why study it: the combat system is built around the Surul Vaal, a flexible ribbon-like blade from South Indian martial traditions, with Kalaripayattu as a movement foundation.
- Action design: wide crowd-clearing arcs operate at range, then the same encounter can transition to close targeted strikes or heavier weapons, making distance a visible combat state.
- Camera / editing: third-person framing keeps the flexible weapon radius, target spacing and protagonist orientation readable through large arcs.
- AI-video takeaway: for flexible or chain weapons, describe the arc radius and distance state explicitly: long-range circular sweep → opponent closes distance → weapon retracts → close targeted strike. Do not prompt the weapon as a generic sword.
- Tags: son-of-thanjai, 2026, kalaripayattu, surul-vaal, flexible-weapon, distance-control, game-combat

- Event page: https://www.onefc.com/events/one-friday-fights-167/
- Type: Martial Arts / Muay Thai
- Date: 2026-08-21
- Why study it: a recent elite rematch that ended in a split decision for Kongsuk, making it useful for studying a competitive fight where small tactical advantages matter more than a single spectacular sequence.
- Action design: use the real-fight rhythm as reference—probe/feint → short committed exchange → defensive response or partial contact → angle change → stance reset.

## Stunt / Choreography

### The Fall Guy — Introducing the Stunt Team
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x5bAHXawDFg
- Type: Stunt / practical-action process
- Why study it: Universal's official featurette foregrounds the stunt performers behind high falls, crashes, explosions, window breaks and close physical action.
- Action design: spectacle is decomposed into repeatable performer tasks with precise launch, impact and recovery moments instead of treated as one vague 'big stunt.'
- Camera / editing: camera position is chosen around the practical stunt so the physical event reads clearly, then coverage and VFX support rather than replace the core motion.
- AI-video takeaway: plan a complex AI stunt as three layers: performer trajectory → impact/contact event → camera path. Add debris, sparks and speed only after those three are unambiguous.

### M3GAN 2.0 — Character-Specific Fight Choreography
- Source / official production feature: https://www.nbcuniversal.com/article/everything-you-need-know-about-m3gan-20
- Type: Stunt / movement design / character choreography
- Why study it: the production deliberately gave M3GAN and Amelia distinct movement systems; Amie Donald trained in Wing Chun, while movement coach Luke Hawker and stunt coordinator Isaac Hamon shaped separate robotic fighting identities.
- Action design: precise close-range martial arts, robotic control and character-specific signature motions are treated as identity rules, not interchangeable moves.

- AI-video takeaway: assign each fighter one movement grammar before writing the exchange—for example A = compact Wing Chun-like precision, B = military/inhuman angular motion—and never let those styles swap after the characters cross positions.

## AI Action

### UniMoCa — Unified Motion + Camera Control for Human Video Generation
- Project / demos: https://tanliming-daniel.github.io/UniMoCa/
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01944
- Type: AI Action / motion-camera control research
- Date: 2026-08-03
- Why study it: the project includes Boxing, Action Scene, Fight, Kick and Kungfu demos and explicitly tests the same motion under static, left, right and upward camera edits.
- Motion design: motion and camera are represented together through an identity-neutral visual proxy, helping separate 'the person moved' from 'the camera moved.'
- Camera / editing: the demo is especially useful for comparing identical action under different camera trajectories while preserving subject layout and timing.
- AI-video takeaway: treat choreography and cinematography as two coordinated control tracks. First lock body interaction and contact timing; then test static/lateral/upward camera variants without changing the fight itself. This is directly applicable to reference-video workflows.
- Tags: unimoca, ai-video, motion-control, camera-control, fight, kungfu, multi-person, 2026
