## Film / TV

### The Bourne Ultimatum — Bourne vs. Desh, Tangier Apartment Fight

- Category: Film / Close-Quarters / Improvised-Weapon Fight
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLt7lXDCHQ0
- Why it is worth studying: A compact, high-pressure fight that feels frantic without becoming spatially meaningless. Both performers repeatedly solve immediate contact problems at arm's length instead of performing long exhibition combos.
- Action design: Collision / clinch entry → short strike or control exchange → improvised-object or weapon response → angle reversal → hard environmental contact → rapid recovery and re-entry. Attacks stay causally linked to the opponent's last action.
- Camera / editing: Handheld close coverage increases urgency, but the camera repeatedly re-establishes torso orientation and the current contact line before the next burst. Cuts are fast without fully abandoning the room axis.
- AI-video takeaways: Useful for making aggressive handheld AI fights readable. Keep action in 1–2 second causal phrases, preserve which hand controls the weapon or limb, and use camera shake as texture rather than as a substitute for choreography.

### Naruto Shippuden — Sasuke vs. Killer Bee

- Category: Anime / Multi-Weapon Duel / Team Interruption
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FfQ1g0e61JA
- Episode: https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/GYK5PKEPR/the-eight-tails-vs-sasuke
- Why it is worth studying: Killer Bee's seven-sword style creates unusually complex attack vectors, yet the scene remains readable because attacks are grouped into short bursts with strong body silhouettes and clear defensive reactions.
- Action design: Unorthodox multi-angle sword entry → Sasuke evades or blocks under pressure → brief reset / reposition → another burst from a new angle → teammate interruption or tactical state change. Bee's weapon language stays distinct from Sasuke's throughout.
- Camera / editing: Rapid cuts and perspective changes are anchored by strong poses, weapon direction, and clear displacement, so speed does not erase the attacker-defender relationship.
- AI-video takeaways: Excellent reference for asymmetric combat styles. Give each fighter a different movement vocabulary, define only 3–5 anchor states, and let fast motion connect them. For extra participants, use one short intervention at a time rather than turning the shot into a simultaneous swarm.

## Prompt patterns distilled from this batch

- **Long-weapon mechanics:** stance / weight shift → rear-hand drive and front-hand guide → visible weapon-tip path → directional change → stable recovery.
- **Aggressive handheld CQC:** contact problem → short causal response → environmental or improvised-object beat → angle reversal → recovery; camera shake stays secondary to body orientation.
- **Side-view combat test:** fixed screen direction → one active target → compact attack phrase → displacement → reset → next target. Add cinematic camera only after the action reads cleanly.
- **Grounded weapon duel:** measure → telegraph → defensive read → contact → punish → separation / guard reset.
- **Asymmetric anime duel:** distinct movement vocabularies → 3–5 anchor poses → speed bridge → clear displacement → one timed third-party intervention.
- **Reference-driven stunt phrase:** preserve start pose, body trajectory, contact timing, and end pose; change identity / set / rendering separately.
- **AI multi-enemy battle:** lock one hero → treat crowd as directional pressure → alternate fast burst and readable impact hold → re-establish camera and identity before the next burst.
