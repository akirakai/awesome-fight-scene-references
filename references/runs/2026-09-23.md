Six new references selected for practical AI-video use. This batch emphasizes readable body mechanics, motivated camera movement, state changes, spatial anchors, and references that can be split into short motion-control clips.

## Martial Arts / Performance

- Primary source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6IqFQL1zhhI
- Official event/results context: https://www.iwuf.org/en/news/gjwl/2026/0708/10525.html
- Why it is worth studying: Elite long-fist routines expose full-body acceleration, aerial difficulty, landing control, extension, and recovery without cinematic editing hiding the mechanics. IWUF reported Yang Yalin winning the men’s Changquan title at the 4th Taolu World Cup with 9.803.
- Action design: grounded preparation → explosive traveling combination → jump / rotation → controlled landing → immediate directional change → stable finishing line.

- AI-video takeaways: excellent reference for aerial attacks and acrobatic transitions. Explicitly preserve takeoff leg, hip rotation, airborne pose, landing foot, balance recovery, and final facing direction; add cinematic camera movement only after the motion is stable.

## Film / TV

### Tom-Yum-Goong / The Protector — Four-Minute Staircase Fight

- Category: Film / Muay Thai / One-Take / One-vs-Many
- Primary source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bqw369ZskMk
- Alternate watch page: https://watch.plex.tv/sv/movie/the-protector-2005
- Why it is worth studying: One of the clearest examples of a long action scene whose geography remains readable while the hero keeps moving through multiple floors. The sequence is famous for its roughly four-minute Steadicam long take.
- Action design: advance to a new spatial station → one active attacker commits → short decisive counter / throw → attacker exits frame → hero resumes upward movement → next threat enters.
- Camera / editing: continuous forward-and-upward tracking; the staircase, landings, railings, doorways, and floor levels act as persistent spatial anchors instead of relying on cuts.
- AI-video takeaways: ideal template for a 10–15 second one-vs-many shot. Use 2–3 spatial stations, one active attacker at a time, one queued threat, and a clearly defined exit path. Let environmental progression motivate the camera.
- Tags: muay-thai, one-take, tracking-shot, one-vs-many, staircase, spatial-continuity

## Games

### Final Fantasy VII Revelation — State of Play Combat & Exploration Deep Dive

- Category: Games / Action RPG / Party Combat / State Change
- Primary source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kSdslzdqWPI
- Official showcase index: https://www.playstation.com/en-tw/state-of-play/
- Why it is worth studying: The September 2026 gameplay deep dive shows a combat system where party synergies, traversal, and the FITS tactical-outfit system visibly change how characters move and attack, rather than treating power-ups as cosmetic effects.
- Action design: approach / traversal → enemy telegraph → character-specific attack → party synergy → visible role or outfit-state change → different follow-up vocabulary → reposition.
- Camera / editing: third-person game camera preserves target relation and arena geography; spectacular attacks are framed by readable setup and recovery states.
- AI-video takeaways: use it as a state-machine reference. When a character changes mode, also change stance, attack cadence, movement distance, and effect language. Keep the target location and screen direction stable across the transformation.
- Tags: ff7, action-rpg, synergy, transformation, state-machine, third-person

## Anime

### Samurai Champloo — Jin vs. Kariya Kagetoki

- Category: Anime / Sword Duel / Minimalist Samurai Fight
- Direct fight clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AXfoOrAwhT4
- Official episode source (E25): https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/G6X02E25Y/evanescent-encounter-part-2
- Official episode source (E26): https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/GY19GVG7R/evanescent-encounter-part-3
- Why it is worth studying: The duel is useful because speed comes from timing, commitment, and sudden changes of line rather than nonstop visual noise. Clean silhouettes and momentary pauses make the sword logic legible.
- Action design: measure / guard → committed cut → evade or deflect → angle change → short counter → separation → re-read opponent → decisive re-entry.
- Camera / editing: restrained framing and short cuts preserve blade direction and facing. Stillness before a burst increases the perceived speed of the attack.
- AI-video takeaways: strong template for 6–12 second samurai duels. Define 3–5 anchor poses and exact facing directions; use brief acceleration between anchors rather than continuous random sword motion.
- Tags: samurai-champloo, sword-duel, timing, silhouette, anchor-pose, anime

## Stunt / Choreography

### LBP / Asylum Stunt Practice with Andy Long and Jim Ng

- Category: Stunt / Fight Practice / Group Fight / One-Take
- Source: https://vimeo.com/9666513
- Why it is worth studying: The rehearsal explicitly includes partner fights, group fights, and long one-take sections, with choreography by Emmanuel Manzanares and Andreas Nguyen. Because it is practice footage, the movement logic is exposed without production design or VFX hiding mistakes.
- Action design: lead exchange → attacker displacement → target handoff → queued attacker enters → short group phrase → separation / reset.
- Camera / editing: rehearsal coverage favors full-body readability and longer takes; it is easy to see spacing, safe contact distance, performer timing, and where each attacker waits before entering.
- AI-video takeaways: excellent raw V2V reference. Crop 2–5 second phrases and let the source control body trajectory, contact timing, camera side, and ending pose. Keep “one active attacker + one queued attacker” as a hard constraint.
- Tags: stunt-practice, group-fight, choreography, one-take, v2v, spacing

## AI Action

### AI Original Combat Short “FIGHT” — Updream / Seedance / MiniMax H3

- Category: AI Action / Multi-Shot Fight Short / Edit Design
- Source: https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1rh8361EWc/
- Published: 2026-08-18
- Why it is worth studying: The creator describes starting from many AI fight tests, then redesigning them into a coherent one-minute action short; most shots were made with Updream, with the ending title generated locally using MiniMax H3. It is therefore useful as an example of building continuity from short AI-generated modules instead of demanding one generation solve the whole scene.
- Action design: short self-contained action beat → explicit exit pose / direction → cut → next beat inherits the state → escalation → final resolution.
- Camera / editing: continuity is constructed editorially across multiple generated shots; each shot carries only a limited action burden.
- AI-video takeaways: treat complex fights as 2–5 second modules. For each module define one main action, one opponent response, one camera instruction, and a precise end state. Track identity, weapon state, screen direction, and injury / costume continuity across cuts.
