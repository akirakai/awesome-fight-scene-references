## Martial Arts

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Nandao

- Category: Martial Arts / Southern Broadsword
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6NtiK_yy6Y
- Source: WUSHU TV (IWUF WUSHU OFFICIAL), 17th World Wushu Championships.
- Why it is worth studying: clean competition coverage exposes the complete body-to-weapon motion chain without cinematic edits hiding footwork or balance.
- Action design: rooted low stances, explosive direction changes, stamping accents, torso rotation and committed broadsword cuts alternate with brief stabilization beats.
- Camera / editing: neutral full-body framing keeps feet, hips, shoulders, hands and blade path visible together.
- AI-video takeaway: prompt the body mechanics first — stance/weight shift → hip-and-shoulder rotation → arm/blade arc → foot landing → recovery. Add cloth, hair, dust and cinematic camera motion only after the blade path is stable.
- Good 15s pattern: grounded opening → two explosive cuts → low stance transition → turning slash → stable recovery.
- Tags: nandao, wushu, broadsword, full-body-reference, weapon-trajectory, grounded-footwork

## Film / TV

### Oldboy — Hallway Fight

- Category: Film / One-vs-Many Corridor Fight
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvQ7Z6ZCxTc
- Director breakdown: https://www.vanityfair.com/video/watch/notes-on-a-scene-park-chan-wook-breaks-down-oldboy-fight-scenes
- Why it is worth studying: one of the clearest examples of a one-axis group fight where exhaustion, crowd pressure and geography remain readable.
- Action design: attackers enter in waves rather than all acting at once; the protagonist repeatedly strikes, absorbs impact, stumbles, recovers and continues advancing through the corridor.
- Camera / editing: the famous lateral presentation behaves almost like a side-scrolling game, preserving the corridor axis and keeping the active attacker readable.
- AI-video takeaway: for one-vs-many AI fights, keep one active attacker plus one nearby interrupter at a time. Use fatigue and recovery as explicit beats instead of generating uninterrupted perfect combos.
- Good 15s pattern: advance → attacker A exchange → stumble/recover → attacker B enters → wall/floor interaction → continue moving.
- Tags: oldboy, hallway, one-vs-many, lateral-camera, fatigue, wave-staging, classic

### Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon — Shu Lien vs. Jen Weapon-Rack Duel

- Category: Film / Wuxia Weapon Duel
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9OxQ-2gR1DU
- Canonical film source: https://www.sonypictures.com/movies/crouchingtigerhiddendragon
- Why it is worth studying: the duel turns weapon choice itself into choreography; range, grip, rhythm and posture visibly change whenever Shu Lien switches weapons against the Green Destiny.
- Action design: weapon state → short exchange → weapon failure/disarm → immediate transition to a new weapon; Jen's superior sword creates a consistent tactical rule across the whole sequence.
- Camera / editing: medium-wide coverage prioritizes full weapon arcs and pair spacing, with closer cuts reserved for impact or tactical change.
- AI-video takeaway: explicitly declare weapon-state transitions. Never let weapons morph between frames: current weapon → visible break/drop → reach/grab next weapon → new movement vocabulary.
- Good 15s pattern: spear/staff exchange → weapon breaks → visible pickup → short blade exchange → displacement/recovery.
- Tags: crouching-tiger-hidden-dragon, michelle-yeoh, zhang-ziyi, wuxia, weapon-switching, swordfight, classic

## Games

### Crimson Desert — Hexe Marie Boss Fight

- Category: Games / Human vs. Supernatural Boss
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W8dvGY0fhwY
- Boss reference: https://www.rpgsite.net/news/16632-crimson-desert-unravels-boss-battle-against-hexe-marie
- Why it is worth studying: the encounter alternates direct boss pressure, summons, ranged threats and teleport/reposition states instead of presenting a single repeated melee loop.
- Action design: boss telegraph → hero dodge/reposition → short punish window → boss relocates or summons adds → hero clears/avoids the secondary threat → re-engage.
- Camera / editing: third-person combat framing keeps the hero orientation and boss location readable even when effects and additional enemies increase visual density.
- AI-video takeaway: model a boss fight as a visible state machine. Define the boss state change before adding VFX: telegraph → attack → reaction → relocation/summon → new attack window.
- Good 15s pattern: boss telegraph → sideways dodge → 2-hit punish → teleport endpoint revealed → minion interruption → hero reorients to boss.
- Tags: crimson-desert, hexe-marie, boss-fight, teleport, summons, state-machine, 2026

## Anime

### BAKI-DOU — Main PV

- Category: Anime / Sword vs. Bare-Hand Fighters
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-BsvEK0gCCs
- Source: TMS Anime official channel / Netflix anime promotion, published February 4, 2026.
- Why it is worth studying: Miyamoto Musashi's sword vocabulary is deliberately contrasted with fighters who rely on fists, grappling and raw physical pressure, creating highly readable asymmetry.
- Action design: weapon threat establishes distance → bare-hand fighter commits to entry → decisive pose/contact → displacement or reset; each fighter keeps a distinctive movement grammar.
- Camera / editing: strong silhouettes and impact poses anchor rapid cuts, while the blade direction remains visually obvious before effects or reaction shots take over.
- AI-video takeaway: when fighters use different systems, assign each one a fixed action vocabulary. Do not let the bare-hand fighter suddenly inherit sword-like motion or let the swordsman become generic brawler motion.
- Good 15s pattern: sword guard → opponent closes distance → single clash/avoidance → heavy counter pose → separation → second committed entry.
- Tags: baki-dou, miyamoto-musashi, anime-fight, sword-vs-fists, asymmetric-combat, 2026

### Star Wars: Visions Presents — The Ninth Jedi: Scene Breakdown

- Category: Anime / Lightsaber Action
- Watch: https://www.starwars.com/video/scene-breakdown-star-wars-visions-presents-the-ninth-jedi
- Series / trailer hub: https://www.starwars.com/series/star-wars-visions-presents-the-ninth-jedi
- Why it is worth studying: the official 2:20 scene breakdown is a compact, directly watchable reference focused on Kara's combat skills rather than a montage-only trailer.
- Action design: strong guard silhouettes, committed blade paths, evasive displacement and counters are separated into readable beats before the sequence accelerates.
- Camera / editing: animated camera movement can be aggressive, but blade direction and character orientation are re-established at each major attack/counter beat.
- AI-video takeaway: for glowing-blade fights, solve body positions and blade trajectories before adding bloom, sparks or energy trails. Effects should follow the blade rather than replace its physical path.

## Stunt / Choreography

### Kevin Inouye — Sword & Melee Weapons Stunt Reel

- Category: Stunt / Weapon Choreography
- Watch: https://vimeo.com/750929811
- Why it is worth studying: the reel samples multiple practical weapon systems — medieval swords, rapier/dagger, smallsword, katana, knife, tomahawk and more — with a trained stunt performer whose grip and range change with the weapon.
- Action design: each weapon has its own stance width, guard, reach, hand relationship and recovery path instead of being treated as the same generic 'sword fighting' motion.
- Camera / editing: reel-style coverage exposes short complete action phrases, making it easy to isolate 2–5 second motion references for AI video.
- AI-video takeaway: bind weapon identity to movement grammar. A rapier phrase, katana phrase and broadsword phrase should not share the same footwork or hand path; keep the chosen weapon fixed for the whole generated beat.
- Good 15s pattern: choose one weapon only → guard/measure → attack/parry → angle change → counter → recovery.
- Tags: stunt-reel, weapon-choreography, sword, rapier, katana, knife, motion-reference

- Category: AI Action / Martial-Arts Generation Benchmark
