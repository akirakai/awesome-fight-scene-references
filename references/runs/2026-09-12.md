# Fight Scene Reference Batch — 2026-09-12

Seven newly screened references for AI-video action design. This batch was checked against the repository's existing titles/keywords before inclusion.

## Martial Arts

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Taijijian & Jianshu Session

- Category: Martial Arts / Performance / Sword Forms
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA_HXqJG9OY
- Why it is worth studying: An IWUF/WUSHU TV official competition recording with minimal cinematic interference, making footwork, stance depth, body-to-blade timing, direction changes, and recovery poses easy to inspect.
- Action design: Taijijian emphasizes continuous weight transfer and blade-path continuity; Jianshu adds sharper acceleration, stops, jumps, and directional switches. Both are useful for learning how the torso and hips drive the weapon rather than letting the sword move independently.
- Camera / editing: Mostly stable competition coverage with the full body and weapon visible, so the movement can be extracted without compensating for aggressive cuts.
- AI-video takeaways: Use as clean motion reference for sword users. Prompt the body chain explicitly: weight shift → hip/shoulder turn → blade arc → foot replacement → recovery pose. Add cinematic camera movement only after motion fidelity is stable.
- Tags: wushu, taijijian, jianshu, sword-form, full-body, weapon-trajectory, motion-reference

## Film / TV

### Chocolate (2008) — Candy Factory Brawl

- Category: Film / TV / One-vs-Many Martial Arts
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qPVePtS52Gc
- Why it is worth studying: A strong JeeJa Yanin/Panna Rittikrai-era example of fast female martial-arts action where the protagonist's body stays readable despite multiple opponents and dense environmental hazards.
- Action design: The fight is built from short local problems: one opponent enters, Zen solves the exchange with a compact kick/strike sequence, then immediately reorients toward the next threat. Environment and opponent spacing constantly change the available movement vocabulary.
- Camera / editing: Medium and medium-wide framings prioritize full-body kicks and clear contact. Cuts generally follow target changes or location changes rather than hiding every strike.
- AI-video takeaways: Good one-vs-many template. Keep one active attacker plus one queued threat, preserve a clear body orientation after each beat, and use the environment to motivate the transition to the next opponent.
- Tags: chocolate, jeeja-yanin, thai-action, one-vs-many, female-fighter, environmental-action, target-handoff

### Crazy Samurai: 400 vs 1 — Long-Take Samurai Attrition

- Category: Film / TV / Samurai / Long Take
- Source: https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/crazy_samurai_400_vs_1/videos
- Why it is worth studying: An extreme long-take experiment centered on Tak Sakaguchi moving through waves of sword-wielding opponents. Its value is less about individual flashy moves and more about sustaining spatial continuity, fatigue, target turnover, and forward progression over a very long fight.
- Action design: Opponents arrive in waves and are resolved through compact attack-response phrases; the protagonist repeatedly resets stance, changes direction, advances, and conserves motion rather than performing a fresh complex combo against every enemy.
- Camera / editing: The moving camera stays physically attached to the progression of the fight, using travel, terrain changes, and pauses as natural punctuation instead of relying on conventional montage.
- AI-video takeaways: Excellent reference for designing a 15-second "oner" as 3–4 spatial stations: enter zone → solve one opponent → move/reorient → solve next opponent. Do not fill every frame with micro-combos; allow locomotion and recovery to carry continuity.
- Tags: crazy-samurai, tak-sakaguchi, oner, swordfight, wave-based-combat, spatial-continuity, fatigue

## Games

### Echoes of Aincrad — Gameplay Trailer / Partner Switch Combat

- Category: Games / Real-Time Sword Combat / Two-Character Coordination
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WFmfiHCKtD8
- Official combat-system notes: https://www.bandainamcoent.com/news/echoes-of-aincrad-reveals-combat-system-and-signature-gameplay-features
- Why it is worth studying: The combat explicitly revolves around a partner and a Switch system, making it a useful reference for two-character AI choreography where allies should cooperate instead of both attacking independently.
- Action design: Player attack → partner handoff/special skill → role switch → continuation of the chain. The tactical identity of the active attacker remains clear, and each handoff creates a natural beat boundary.
- Camera / editing: Third-person action coverage keeps the current attacker, target, and partner relationship visible enough to understand who owns the next beat.
- AI-video takeaways: For duo fights, write the prompt as role handoffs rather than simultaneous action: Character A commits → Character B intercepts/extends → target reacts → Character A or B becomes the new active attacker. This reduces identity and causality errors.
- Tags: echoes-of-aincrad, sword-art-online, partner-system, switch-system, duo-combat, handoff, game-choreography

## Anime

### Sword of the Stranger — Nanashi vs. Luo-Lang Final Duel

- Category: Anime / Sword Duel
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JP5uSX1UUtg
- Full film / canonical stream: https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/G31UXJW8E/sword-of-the-stranger
- Why it is worth studying: One of animation's clearest high-speed sword-duel references: speed is extreme, but blade intent, body orientation, misses, clashes, displacement, and recovery remain legible.
- Action design: The sequence repeatedly cycles through guard/read → committed cut → evade/parry → separation → rapid re-entry. The fighters have distinct rhythm and spacing rather than performing mirrored choreography.
- Camera / editing: Dynamic tracking and angle changes accelerate the fight, but decisive clashes repeatedly return to readable silhouettes and strong screen direction.
- AI-video takeaways: Lock only 4–6 anchor states in a short generation—guard, entry, clash, displacement, recovery, finishing attempt—then let smear, motion blur, snow/debris, and camera movement create the perceived speed between them.
- Tags: sword-of-the-stranger, anime, swordfight, sakuga, anchor-poses, high-speed-action, screen-direction

## Stunt / Choreography

### Jumper — Experimental Sci-Fi Fight Choreography / Camera-Tech Study

- Category: Stunt / Choreography / Experimental Camera
- Source: https://vimeo.com/1114266938
- Why it is worth studying: A modern experimental action short built specifically around fight choreography plus motion-control arm shots, point-cloud transitions, a 45-camera 360° bullet-time rig, POV, and transition design.
- Action design: Physical stunt exchanges remain the underlying source of truth; the advanced capture techniques are layered around those exchanges rather than replacing them.
- Camera / editing: Especially useful for studying when to switch from normal tracking to POV, robotic motion-control moves, freeze/bullet-time, and digital spatial transitions while preserving the same action beat.
- AI-video takeaways: Generate the choreography first, then stylize one transition at a time. A practical 15-second structure is normal-speed setup → clear contact beat → one bullet-time/POV transformation → return to normal-speed recovery. Avoid stacking every camera trick into every beat.
- Tags: jumper, stunt-choreography, motion-control, bullet-time, pov, 360-camera, sci-fi-action, camera-design
