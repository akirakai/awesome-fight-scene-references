## Martial Arts

### ONE Friday Fights 171 — Klarob NuiCafeboran vs. Sornsueknoi FA Group

- Category: Martial Arts / Muay Thai / Real Combat
- Watch: https://www.dailymotion.com/video/xb9q4be
- Official result / breakdown: https://www.onefc.com/news/one-friday-fights-171-main-event-result-klarob-outlasts-sornsueknoi-to-claim-stunning-victory/
- Date: September 18, 2026
- Why it is worth studying: A very fresh elite-level fight with unusually clear examples of range management, guarded or partially absorbed attacks, hurt-and-recovery moments, counter punching and close-range exchanges. It is useful precisely because not every strike lands cleanly.
- Action design: Right high-kick pressure into the guard → boxing response → short reset → heavy hook / visible balance disruption → close-range uppercut-and-hook return fire → elbow at the end of a punching phrase → renewed pressure and recovery.
- Camera / editing: Broadcast coverage stays wide enough to preserve both fighters, their stance, distance and the contact line; the camera does not need to manufacture speed.
- AI-video takeaways: For believable striking, explicitly include blocked kicks, partial contacts, brief hurt states, guard recovery and foot repositioning. A useful 8–12 second grammar is probe → counter → visible reaction → short return burst → reset, rather than making every attack land cleanly.
- Tags: muay-thai, real-fight, range-management, counter, partial-contact, guard-reset, 2026

## Film / TV

### Drunken Master II (1994) — Final Ironworks Fight

- Category: Film / Kung Fu / Character-Specific Choreography
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NJILX-ummwE
- Additional production context: https://www.neatorama.com/2025/07/16/Chaos-in-a-Factory-the-Drunken-Master-II-Final-Fight/
- Why it is worth studying: The finale is a masterclass in giving two opponents completely different movement grammars. Wong Fei-hung’s loose, low, apparently unstable drunken-boxing rhythm is contrasted with Ken Lo’s upright, fast, long-line kicking style, so the characters remain identifiable through movement alone.
- Action design: Structured technical exchange → repeated low-line evasions and off-balance-looking recoveries → opponent’s long kicking pressure → environmental displacement around the ironworks → explicit escalation into a more extreme drunken-boxing state.
- Camera / editing: Medium and wide coverage repeatedly returns to full-body readability, letting footwork, kick extension, falls and recoveries carry the spectacle. Cuts punctuate tactical changes instead of hiding body mechanics.
- AI-video takeaways: Assign each fighter one stable movement vocabulary before writing the combo. For a 15-second homage, use three phases: clean style contrast → environmental disruption → explicit style-state escalation. “Unstable-looking” motion still needs a credible center of gravity and recovery path.
- Tags: jackie-chan, drunken-boxing, taekwondo, style-contrast, full-body, environmental-action, classic

## Games

### Wo Long 2: Wings of Ember — Release Date Trailer / Alpha Demo Combat

- Category: Games / Chinese Martial Arts / Deflect-Counter Combat
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6e6lN24jpA0
- Official combat / demo notes: https://blog.playstation.com/2026/09/15/wo-long-2-wings-of-ember-launches-march-4-2027-demo-live-today/
- Date: September 15, 2026
- Why it is worth studying: The current Alpha Demo is explicitly built around a flow between offense and defense. Deflect redirects committed attacks, while Insight Skills can counter, drive enemies into walls, or slam airborne attackers into the ground. That makes the footage a clean model for causal action instead of simultaneous move spam.
- Action design: Enemy telegraph / commitment → precise Deflect → short counter window → Insight Skill → terrain-aware displacement such as wall impact or aerial slam → recovery into the next neutral state.
- Camera / editing: Stable third-person combat framing preserves the enemy wind-up, defensive timing and result of the counter even when effects become dense.
- AI-video takeaways: Treat a duel as a state machine: telegraph → defensive read → contact/redirect → one counter → environmental consequence → reset. Keep terrain interaction as the result of a visible hit, not an independent VFX event.
- Tags: wo-long-2, team-ninja, deflect, counter, chinese-martial-arts, terrain-impact, state-machine, 2026

## Anime

### Naruto — Rock Lee vs. Gaara

- Category: Anime / Speed Escalation / Asymmetric Duel
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pi6ICtn9S4U
- Series source: Crunchyroll, Naruto episodes 48–50
- Why it is worth studying: A classic example of making extreme speed readable through explicit state changes. Lee’s physical speed escalates in visible steps, while Gaara’s largely stationary sand defense gives the sequence a stable spatial reference and a very different defensive vocabulary.
- Action design: Weighted / restrained stance → explicit speed-state change → repeated burst entries around a relatively fixed defender → automatic defense begins to fail → vertical / aerial signature attack → impact and recovery.
- Camera / editing: The sequence repeatedly returns to strong pose anchors and known arena positions before another acceleration. Speed lines, reaction shots and impact frames bridge the fastest movement without abandoning spatial logic.
- AI-video takeaways: Make power-up states change locomotion and timing, not merely aura color. Define four anchor states—baseline, speed burst, defense overwhelmed/repositioned, signature finishing attempt—and let effects connect them.
- Tags: naruto, rock-lee, gaara, anime-fight, speed-state, asymmetric-duel, pose-anchor, classic

## Stunt / Choreography

### SNATCHERS — Action Short Film

- Category: Stunt / Choreography / Short-Form Action
- Watch: https://vimeo.com/1112726652
- Fight choreography: Hoon Park
- Why it is worth studying: A compact action short with explicitly credited fight choreography and stunt-team support, making it a useful lower-noise reference than a VFX-heavy studio sequence when studying performer blocking and timing.
- Action design: Best approached as a source of short, self-contained action phrases rather than as one long move list. Inspect each phrase for initiator → response → contact / avoidance → positional change → recovery before sampling it as reference.
- Camera / editing: The short-film format is useful for comparing physical choreography with the final camera coverage and edit, especially where a cut or reframe is motivated by a change in threat or body position.
- AI-video takeaways: For V2V, crop 2–5 second phrases and let the source control body trajectory, contact timing and camera side. Use the text prompt mainly for identity, costume, environment and the required end state instead of rewriting the choreography.
- Tags: stunt-team, fight-choreography, vimeo, short-film, blocking, v2v-reference, modular-action
