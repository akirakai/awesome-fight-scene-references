## Film / TV

### BEAST (2026) — Title Match / Distinct Fighter Grammars
- Watch: https://www.fandango.com/movie-trailer/beast-2026/244538
- Fight-design interview: https://www.rsclfilms.com/news/how-to-build-a-fight-scene-with-beast-star-bren-foster-rscl/
- Why it is worth studying: fight choreographer/star Bren Foster explains how opponents are deliberately given different movement vocabularies instead of sharing one generic martial-arts style.
- Action design: one fighter is built as a compact “warrior tank” centered on hard basic punches and pressure; another is designed as a more flamboyant mover using spins, high kicks and aerial techniques.
- Camera / editing: ring coverage keeps body silhouette, range and style contrast readable while impact shots punctuate the strongest exchanges.
- AI-video takeaway: define each fighter’s movement grammar before describing the fight. Example: Fighter A = forward pressure + heavy boxing; Fighter B = lateral movement + spinning kicks. Identity remains more stable when movement style is as fixed as face and costume.
- Tags: mma, fighter-grammar, contrasting-styles, grounded-action, 2026

### Carter (2022) — Faux One-Cut Action / Seamless Camera Handoffs
- Watch: https://www.netflix.com/title/81399639?clip=81605860&s=a&t=cp&trkid=13747225&vlang=en
- VFX breakdown: https://magicmedia.artstation.com/projects/nEaw5E
- Why it is worth studying: the film is designed to feel like one continuous action shot, making it a useful reference for connecting multiple fight modules without obvious conventional cuts.
- Action design: local encounters are short and goal-driven; the spectacle comes from continuously transitioning into a new spatial/action problem.
- Camera / editing: match-moved 3D cameras, digital transitions, foreground occlusions and aggressive camera travel are used to hide seams between separately captured pieces.
- AI-video takeaway: build a 15-second faux-oner from 3 modules—close exchange → occlusion/whip transition → new threat—rather than asking one generation to invent uninterrupted maximum-complexity motion for the full duration.
- Tags: faux-oner, hidden-cut, camera-handoff, continuous-action, vfx

## Martial Arts

### Yokkiri TN Muaythai vs. Ali Al-Kinani — ONE Friday Fights 167 (2026-08-21)
- Full event replay: https://watch.onefc.com/events/one-friday-fights-167
- Official result/highlights: https://www.onefc.com/news/one-friday-fights-167-co-main-event-result-yokkiris-clinch-work-proves-decisive-against-ali-al-kinani/
- Why it is worth studying: a very recent real fight where tactical adaptation is easy to read—longer-range punches and spinning attacks are progressively answered by shelling, distance closing, clinch control and knees.
- Action design: pressure/jab or spinning attack → defensive shell/dip → distance close → clinch entry → knee sequence → reset. The useful realism comes from adaptation rather than symmetrical trading.
- Camera / editing: broadcast framing keeps both fighters, entry distance and clinch contact visible, making it practical motion reference for AI two-person combat.
- AI-video takeaway: give the fighters different tactical objectives. A attacks from range; B survives, closes distance and changes the fight state. This produces more believable causality than having both characters throw unrelated combinations simultaneously.
- Tags: muay-thai, clinch, tactical-adaptation, knees, realistic-combat, 2026
