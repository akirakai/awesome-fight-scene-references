## Film / TV

### The Man from Nowhere (2010) — Cha Tae-sik vs. Lum Ramrowan Knife Fight

- Category: Film / Knife Fight / Close Quarters
- Watch: https://www.bilibili.tv/en/video/2049659101
- Why it is worth studying: A compact knife/karambit duel in a small tiled room where blade-hand continuity, distance, wounds and counters remain unusually legible.
- Action design: probing blade entry → hand trap/parry → short counter → position change → re-entry from a new angle → increasingly desperate finish.
- Camera / editing: Close-medium framing and relatively long holds keep hands, blades and body orientation readable instead of cutting on every contact.
- AI-video takeaways: Lock each weapon to a specific hand and preserve attack-response causality. A useful 15-second structure is 3–5 anchor exchanges with a short reset or angle change between them.
- Tags: knife-fight, karambit, close-quarters, long-take, weapon-continuity, counter, korean-action

### Police Story 3: Supercop (1992) — Rooftop / Train Action Escalation

- Category: Film / Environmental Stunts / Moving Vehicle
- Watch / source: https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Police+Story+3+Supercop+train+fight+official+clip
- Why it is worth studying: A classic Jackie Chan example of action escalating through changing physical problems—height, speed, moving surfaces and environmental hazards—rather than simply adding more punches.
- Action design: climb/approach → unstable-footing exchange → obstacle avoidance → vehicle-relative movement → recovery into the next hazard.
- Camera / editing: Wider stunt coverage lets the audience understand the real spatial risk; cuts generally follow location or tactical changes rather than disguising movement.
- AI-video takeaways: For vehicle fights, define the platform motion and environmental hazard as first-class constraints. Build the scene as 2–3 spatial stations instead of asking for continuous random combat on a moving train.
- Tags: jackie-chan, train-fight, moving-platform, environmental-action, practical-stunts, classic-action

## AI Action

### The Trigger — Higgsfield Originals

- Category: AI Action / AI Short Film
- Watch: https://higgsfield.ai/original-series/the-trigger/full-film
- Why it is worth studying: A recent long-form AI action short that offers a useful test of whether character, weapon and action state can stay coherent across many separately generated shots rather than only one isolated demo clip.
- Action design: The strongest reusable lesson is modularity—each shot solves one principal action problem before handing off identity, weapon state, screen direction and exit pose to the next shot.
- Camera / editing: Aggressive cinematic camera moves are distributed across cuts instead of forcing every individual shot to contain maximum motion, helping the sequence preserve visual intent over a longer runtime.
- AI-video takeaways: Build complex fights as 2–5 second modules. Give each module one main physical action, one camera instruction and one explicit end state; carry that end state into the next generation.
- Tags: higgsfield, ai-action, ai-short-film, modular-generation, state-handoff, shot-design, 2026

## Batch takeaways

- For grounded combat, the most reusable rule is still **setup → committed action → response/contact → recovery/reset**.
- For weapon fights, preserve **weapon-hand identity, attack vector and contact causality** before adding speed or camera shake.
- For highly stylized anime/AI action, use a small set of **anchor states** and let motion effects connect them.
- For multi-shot AI fights, carry forward **identity, screen direction, weapon state and exit pose** as explicit state between shots.
