- Why it is worth studying: The third One Shot film preserves the franchise’s signature illusion of one continuous take, with the camera following action through rooms, hallways, vehicles, and other connected spaces.
- Action design: Compact hand strikes, elbows, knees, wall/stair transitions, and tactical movement work as local problems inside a larger continuous mission rather than as a disconnected martial-arts showcase.

### Rurouni Kenshin: The Final — Kenshin vs. Enishi Final Duel

- Category: Film / Sword Fight / Wuxia-Influenced Live Action
- Watch: https://www.bilibili.tv/TH/video/2044638446
- Action design interview: https://gigazine.net/news/20210624-rurouni-kenshin-keishi-otomo-kenji-tanigaki-interview/
- Why it is worth studying: Kenji Tanigaki’s action design gives the finale a clear contrast between character-specific sword vocabularies while keeping speed, intent, and emotional pressure readable.
- Action design: Read/guard → committed cut → evade or deflect → angle change → counter → short separation → harder re-entry. The duel escalates through increasingly committed exchanges rather than constant random blade motion.
- Camera / editing: Fast reframing is repeatedly anchored by readable silhouettes, facing direction, and clear blade trajectories. Cuts emphasize a tactical change or impact instead of disguising every contact.
- AI-video takeaways: Give each fighter a distinct movement grammar and keep 4–6 anchor states. Let acceleration, sparks, cloth motion, and blade blur connect those states; do not let weapons morph mid-swing.
- Tags: rurouni-kenshin, kenshin, enishi, swordfight, character-specific-style, anchor-pose, live-action
