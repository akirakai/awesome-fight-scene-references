# Fight Scene Reference Run — 2026-08-22

A curated batch of non-duplicate fight and action references selected for AI video generation. Entries favor readable choreography, useful camera language, clear action/reaction timing, and directly reusable prompting patterns.

## Film / TV

### Nobody 2 — Elevator Fight
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8t1cSIe0tSk
- Production / stunt notes: https://www.universalpicturesathome.com/press-release/nobody-2-press-release
- Category: Film / TV — close-quarters fight
- Why it is worth studying: a very constrained elevator space naturally limits movement and keeps every impact tied to walls, doors and body position.
- Action design: entry → close-range pressure → wall/door contact → role reversal → finish/exit. The small environment turns each beat into a clear tactical problem instead of a freeform combo.
- Camera / editing: the elevator axis and fixed surfaces provide strong spatial anchors, making torso, arm and contact direction easy to follow even when the pace rises.
- AI-video takeaway: for a 10–15 second narrow-space fight, reduce degrees of freedom. Use four explicit beats and let the environment create the transitions; hide at most one cut behind a body crossing or door frame if needed.
- Tags: elevator-fight, narrow-space, close-quarters, environmental-contact, readable-action, 2025

- Why it is worth studying: elite competition footage shows realistic attack selection, failed/partial exchanges, clinch resets and a finish that grows out of repeated body attacks rather than a random spectacular move.

## Stunt / Choreography

### Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga — “Stowaway” Behind the Scenes
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9B_OIdq2_Q
- Category: Stunt / Choreography — moving-vehicle action
- Why it is worth studying: the official behind-the-scenes piece shows how a large moving-platform action sequence is built from practical stunt work, performer safety, vehicle choreography and CGI used as support rather than as a substitute for motion design.
- Action design: rider / vehicle / performer trajectories are planned as coordinated layers; the action stays understandable because each participant has a path and timing responsibility.
- Camera / editing: the camera must preserve a stable relationship to the hero while the vehicle and surrounding threats are also moving, so trajectory planning matters as much as the individual stunt.
- AI-video takeaway: separate a complex chase/fight prompt into three motion plans—hero trajectory, vehicle/platform trajectory and camera trajectory. Lock those first, then add debris, speed, dust and VFX.

- Watch: https://www.netflix.com/tudum/videos/sam-larusso-fights-herself-in-this-cobra-kai-shot-by-shot
- Category: Stunt / Choreography — same-performer dual-role fight
- Why it is worth studying: a rare directly watchable breakdown of a character fighting another version of herself, useful for studying identity separation and shot-by-shot continuity when the same performer occupies both roles.
- Action design: the exchange is easiest to understand when each version has a fixed initiator/responder role per beat instead of both acting independently at once.

## AI Action

### InterCMDM — Block-Causal Diffusion for Autoregressive Human Interaction Generation
- Project / videos: https://yu1ut.com/InterCMDM-HP/
- Category: AI Action — two-person interaction generation
- Why it is worth studying: the 2026 system generates long human interactions in short autoregressive blocks and separates the two participants into causal streams, allowing simultaneous action, reactive response, leader-follower behavior and independent motion.
- Movement design: its demos make interaction mode explicit—one person can lead while the other reacts, both can act together, or roles can change over time—rather than assuming one coordination pattern for the whole sequence.
- Camera / editing: simple research visualization exposes timing, spacing and identity drift without cinematic editing hiding failures.
- AI-video takeaway: plan a fight in 2–4 second causal blocks and choose the coordination mode for each block: A leads/B reacts → simultaneous clash → B leads/A reacts → reset. This is a useful mental model for avoiding two fighters who appear to perform unrelated animations.
- Tags: ai-action, two-person-interaction, causal-generation, autoregressive, leader-follower, reaction, eccv-2026

## Batch takeaway

The strongest reusable pattern in this batch is **constrained causality**: limit the space or the interaction mode, make one action visibly cause the next response, and change state only at explicit beats. For 15-second AI fight videos, this is generally more reliable than increasing move count or camera speed.
