## Martial Arts / Performance

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Gao Haonan Men’s Taijijian Gold Medal Performance
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3K_iuC166MU
- Source: WUSHU TV (IWUF WUSHU OFFICIAL).
- Type: Real martial arts / taijijian sword form
- Why study it: clean competition footage exposes the full movement chain without cinematic cutting hiding footwork, balance, wrist control or sword-tip trajectory.
- Action design: continuous center-of-gravity shifts and spiraling torso mechanics alternate with sudden accelerations, direction changes, balances and precise sword arcs.
- Camera / editing: neutral full-body framing keeps feet, torso, hands and blade visible at once, making it unusually useful for pose extraction or motion-reference conditioning.
- AI-video takeaway: prompt the movement from body to weapon: weight shift → hip/shoulder rotation → wrist articulation → sword-tip arc → step/balance → recovery. Add hair, cloth and cinematic camera movement only after the physical path is stable.
- 15s pattern: slow controlled entry → accelerating circular sword phrase → traveling step/turn → balance or level change → sharp directional cut/thrust → continuous recovery into the next phrase.
- Tags: taijijian, wushu, iwuf, sword-form, weapon-trajectory, balance, full-body-reference, 2025

## Film / TV

### SPL / Kill Zone — Donnie Yen vs. Wu Jing Alley Fight
- Watch: https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x8x327a
- Canonical scene index: https://asia-archive.si.edu/the-art-of-action-25-years-of-hong-kong-fight-scenes/
- Type: Film / baton-vs-knife close-quarters duel
- Why study it: one of the clearest classic examples of assigning each fighter a distinct weapon, range and tactical identity inside a narrow location.
- Action design: police baton versus knife creates a readable chain of committed strike → deflection/trap → counter → angle change. The alley geometry prevents the duel from dissolving into random lateral movement.
- Camera / editing: medium and wider two-shots preserve both weapon hands and the attack line; reframing follows changes in pressure while the alley axis stays legible.
- AI-video takeaway: lock weapon identity, handedness and reach before describing speed. Write every exchange as a paired contact event and keep the camera on one stable side until a motivated angle reversal.
- 15s pattern: knife entry → baton intercept → rapid hand/weapon trapping exchange → short disengage → attacker recommits → decisive counter while both weapons remain visually distinct.
- Tags: spl, kill-zone, donnie-yen, wu-jing, baton, knife, hong-kong-action, weapon-identity, classic

- Why study it: a classic sword fight whose speed is built on readable stance, spacing, committed weapon trajectories and escalating pressure rather than constant effects.

- Type: Stunt rehearsal / 2-vs-1 fight choreography
- Why study it: stripped-down rehearsal footage makes the choreography structure more visible than a finished movie scene: who attacks, who waits, how the hero changes target and how bodies reset for the next phrase.

## Reusable prompting lessons from this batch

1. **Weapon and movement identity first.** A knife fighter, baton fighter, taijijian performer, giant boss and ranged support character each need a stable movement grammar before speed or effects are added.
2. **One active relationship at a time.** Even in 2-vs-1 or 4-vs-boss scenes, keep one primary attacker/defender relationship and make other characters short interventions.
3. **Prompt contact causally.** Commitment → contact/avoidance → body displacement → recovery → next decision is more reliable than a list of spectacular techniques.
4. **Use camera movement to reveal a tactical change.** Target switch, scale change, environment transition or state change are strong reasons to move/reframe; arbitrary camera motion is not.
5. **For 15 seconds, preserve momentum through the last frame.** A strong action reference can end mid-continuation or on a committed beat; it does not need a pose, freeze or slowdown.
