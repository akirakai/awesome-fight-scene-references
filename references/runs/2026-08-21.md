# Curated Fight References — 2026-08-21

Nine new, non-duplicate references selected for practical AI-video action design.

## Film / TV

### Mutiny (2026) — “Face Off” Cargo-Ship Corridor Fight
- Watch: https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/mutiny_2026/videos/0nl9zucLeOC0
- Category: Film / close-quarters
- Why study it: a current example of grounded action in a severely constrained passageway.
- Action design: short punches, elbows, grapples and wall/container impacts form a direct attack-response chain.
- Camera / editing: medium-close coverage stays aligned with the corridor axis and uses the walls as spatial anchors.
- AI-video takeaway: constrain a 15-second fight to one corridor and four beats—entry → wall contact/counter → angle reversal → finish.

### The Furious (2026) — Wide, Long-Take Multi-Fighter Choreography
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Avky8dVaqAI
- Breakdown: https://elementsofmadness.com/2026/06/06/ktanigaki/
- Category: Film / martial-arts ensemble
- Why study it: Kenji Tanigaki deliberately keeps performers visible with wide, clean and relatively long coverage.
- Action design: the five-way finale uses role handoffs among three sides instead of making every fighter attack independently at once.
- Camera / editing: the DP joined stunt rehearsal about six weeks before production so camera timing could be learned with choreography; critical motion is kept near frame center.
- AI-video takeaway: for 3+ fighters, define one active pair plus one interrupter, then change roles only after a clear contact beat.

### The Protector (2005) — Staircase Oner
- Watch: https://www.imdb.com/video/vi646562841/
- Category: Film / classic one-take martial arts
- Why study it: a long vertical fight is made readable by turning architecture into natural checkpoints.
- Action design: new attacker → quick exchange → resolution → reorientation → continue climbing.
- Camera / editing: the moving camera follows the climb while landings and floor changes act as visual resets.
- AI-video takeaway: design an oner as three spatial stations with one short interaction at each station rather than a nonstop stream of moves.

## Games

## Martial Arts / Performance

### ONE SAMURAI 2 — Masaaki Noiri vs. Liu Mengyang II
- Highlights / results: https://www.onefc.com/news/one-samurai-2-results-and-highlights-for-every-match/
- Full event replay: https://watch.onefc.com/events/one-samurai-2
- Category: Martial Arts / kickboxing
- Why study it: recent elite competition footage with authentic probing, defensive reads, stance recovery and a decisive short finish.
- Action design: feint/probe → close distance → short committed combination → defensive response → reset or finish.

## Stunt / Choreography

### Everything Everywhere All at Once — How the Kung Fu Fights Were Shot
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psWUuuoYMy8
- Category: Stunt / choreography breakdown
- Why study it: the stunt team explains how Hong Kong action, breakdancing, parkour, props and a single warehouse location were turned into distinctive fight ideas.
- Action design: one prop or environmental limitation becomes one movement mechanic instead of simply adding more strikes.
- Camera / editing: choreography is built for specific framing and performer capability, preserving full-body readability when the physical idea matters.
- AI-video takeaway: assign one prop/spatial constraint to one action phrase, previs that phrase with the camera, then move to the next idea.

## AI Action

### HumanX — Reactive Fighting Learned from Human Videos
- Project / demos: https://wyhuai.github.io/human-x/
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02473
- Category: AI Action / interaction learning
- Why study it: HumanX turns human video into physically plausible humanoid interaction skills and explicitly evaluates reactive fighting.
- Motion design: interaction is treated as an observed initiator trajectory plus a physically conditioned response rather than two unrelated motion tracks.
- Camera / editing: clean research demos expose timing, spacing, balance and reaction quality before cinematic presentation hides errors.
- AI-video takeaway: use paired causal windows—A commits → B reacts → contact/avoidance changes body state → both recover—then add camera and style afterward.
