## Martial Arts

### 11th Sanda World Cup — Day 2 Session 4 Finals
- Category: Martial Arts / Sanda / Real Competition
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vkGnRAQzHEg
- Official context: https://www.iwuf.org/en/news/gjwl/2026/0517/10446.html
- Date: 2026-05-14 broadcast; event held 2026-05-13 to 2026-05-15 in Macao.
- Why it is worth studying: This is a clean real-combat baseline for how trained fighters actually manage distance, feints, committed entries, kick-to-punch transitions, catches, off-balancing, and resets under pressure.
- Action design: Probe at range → one fighter commits → defender checks/counters or closes distance → brief clinch/takedown attempt → both reset to stance. The pauses are useful because they expose the true rhythm behind believable combat.
- Camera / editing: Broadcast coverage stays wide and neutral, keeping both fighters' feet, hips, guard, and ring position readable. There is almost no cinematic cheating.
- AI-video takeaways: Use this as a physics and timing reference before adding cinematic camera motion. For grounded AI fights, prompt explicit attack-response-reset phrases rather than nonstop combinations, and preserve missed attacks, defensive footwork, and weight transfer.
- Tags: sanda, real-combat, footwork, distance-management, takedown, grounded-action, 2026

## Film / TV

### The Raid 2 — Kitchen Fight
- Category: Film / TV / Silat / Close-Quarters Duel
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hwZ1G9fKPI8
- Alternate watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oJPCpMkmBWo
- Why it is worth studying: One of the clearest examples of a fight escalating through increasingly committed exchanges without losing spatial readability. The duel constantly alternates between attack, interception, clinch, separation, and renewed pressure.
- Action design: Short silat combinations → limb control → counter → body collision / environmental contact → disengage → faster re-entry. Damage and fatigue accumulate instead of resetting after every beat.
- Camera / editing: Medium and medium-wide framing preserves full-body mechanics and weapon-hand relationships. Cuts usually happen on tactical transitions, not on every hit, so the viewer can see cause and effect.
- AI-video takeaways: Excellent template for a 15-second duel: 3–4 second exchange → half-second separation → stronger counter-exchange → environmental contact → unresolved forward pressure at the cut. Keep one attacker and one defender active at a time and show contact before reaction.
- Tags: raid-2, silat, kitchen-fight, duel, close-quarters, sustained-takes, escalation

### The Night Comes for Us — Joe Taslim vs. Iko Uwais Final Fight
- Category: Film / TV / Silat / Brutal Duel
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ihPnDVMeEsQ
- Why it is worth studying: The scene is especially useful for studying how two similarly skilled fighters trade initiative. Neither character dominates permanently; momentum changes through small positional wins, fatigue, and increasingly desperate counters.
- Action design: Pressure burst → hard counter → stumble/recovery → renewed close-range exchange → improvised weapon or environmental beat → role reversal. The choreography leaves visible recovery time after major impacts.
- Camera / editing: Mostly readable two-person coverage, with closer framing reserved for major contact or emotional intensity. The scene repeatedly re-establishes geography after violent bursts.
- AI-video takeaways: When prompting a high-intensity duel, explicitly alternate initiative instead of writing one uninterrupted hero combo. Add fatigue and recovery states so the fight feels cumulative rather than game-like.
- Tags: night-comes-for-us, silat, role-reversal, fatigue, brutal-action, duel, impact

## Games

### Phantom Blade Zero — State of Play Gameplay Deep Dive
- Category: Games / Wuxia / Action RPG
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blh1v1dGJ2s
- Official breakdown: https://blog.playstation.com/2026/08/17/phantom-blade-zero-state-of-play-dives-deep-into-combat-and-the-wulin-world/
- Date: 2026-08-17.
- Why it is worth studying: A current high-value reference for translating authentic kung-fu and wuxia timing into stylized game combat. The deep dive shows how weapon identity, parries, counters, special tools, and short movement bursts can stay readable even at high speed.
- Action design: Committed weapon attack → timed defense/parry → clear counter window → short reposition → weapon/tool transition → renewed pressure. Different weapons imply different reach, tempo, and body mechanics instead of functioning as cosmetic swaps.
- Camera / editing: Third-person camera keeps the player and threat relationship stable, while VFX accents the contact frame rather than replacing it. Fast animation is still anchored by visible anticipation and recovery poses.
- AI-video takeaways: For wuxia AI video, define each weapon transition as an explicit state change and preserve anticipation/contact/recovery. Use VFX after the physical beat, not as a substitute for choreography. Good reference for 10–15 second sword or polearm battles with cinematic camera tracking.

## Anime

### Naruto Shippuden — Kakashi vs. Obito
- Category: Anime / Hand-to-Hand / Duel
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/live/Gi8jxlEiQ50
- Source channel: Crunchyroll en Español.
- Why it is worth studying: A benchmark for action storytelling without overloading the frame. The fight uses compact hand-to-hand exchanges, weapon transitions, and mirrored past/present movement to communicate history and emotion while maintaining precise choreography.
- Action design: Probe → parry → short combination → grapple / weapon transition → separation → mirrored re-entry. Movements are economical and each counter grows directly from the previous body position.
- Camera / editing: Tight cuts are motivated by contact, angle change, or the match-cut relationship between past and present. The sequence frequently returns to readable medium shots before another fast burst.
- AI-video takeaways: Very strong template for emotional duels. Anchor the sequence with 4–6 distinct poses and let fast in-between motion connect them. If using flashbacks or visual match cuts, match body orientation and limb position so the transition feels intentional rather than random.

- Why it is worth studying: This is close to the raw material AI creators actually need: sword action stripped of finished-film lighting, VFX, and narrative distractions. You can clearly see spacing, attack lines, partner timing, and how performers move for camera.

## Reusable prompt patterns extracted from this batch

### Grounded duel
`probe / footwork → committed attack → visible defense → contact → body displacement → recovery → counter → brief reset`

### High-speed sword fight
`clear stance → one readable attack line → blade contact/parry → angle change → short reposition → counterattack → continuous forward momentum`

### Multi-source AI workflow
`motion reference controls choreography + character reference controls identity + environment reference controls space + text prompt controls timing, camera intent, and style`

### Wide-shot stability rule
`the wider the shot and the more active fighters present, the simpler each simultaneous action should be; move closer for dense contact choreography`
