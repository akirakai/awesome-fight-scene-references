## Martial Arts

### UFC 331 — Joshua Van vs. Alexandre Pantoja 2

- Category: Martial Arts / MMA / Real Combat
- Watch / highlights: https://www.mmafighting.com/ufc/511352/joshua-van-vs-alexandre-pantoja-full-fight-video-highlights-ufc-331
- Official result: https://www.ufc.com/news/cryptocom-ufc-331-van-vs-pantoja-2-results
- Date: September 19, 2026
- Why it is worth studying: A fresh five-round title fight with unusually useful transitions between striking, takedown attempts, scrambles, clinch control and resets. The value for AI is that the fight contains failed takedowns, reversals, partially blocked attacks and tactical adaptation instead of a continuous clean-hit combo.
- Action design: Aggressive entry / body lock → takedown → scramble back to standing → boxing exchange → leg-kick setup → counter combinations → stuffed takedown / reversal → final-round knockdown and follow-up.
- Camera / editing: Broadcast coverage stays wide enough to preserve both fighters, the fence, stance and range. Replays are separate from the live action, so the primary fight grammar remains easy to trace.
- AI-video takeaways: For believable MMA, explicitly prompt failed entries and recovery: shoot / clinch attempt → defend → re-square → short striking phrase → reset. Keep grappling consequences visible before cutting back to striking.
- Tags: ufc-331, mma, real-fight, takedown-defense, scramble, counter-boxing, range-management, 2026

## Film / TV

### The Raid 2 — Rama vs. The Assassin, Kitchen Fight

- Category: Film / Silat / Close-Quarters Duel
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dij09jG-VEk
- Why it is worth studying: One of the clearest modern examples of escalating a long hand-to-hand duel without losing spatial readability. Rama and The Assassin repeatedly move through short attack-response phrases, separate, re-enter and increase intensity while remaining physically grounded.
- Action design: Guard / measure → short silat entry → parry / limb control → counter → body displacement → separation → harder re-entry → weapon escalation → exhaustion-heavy finishing exchange.
- Camera / editing: Medium and medium-wide framing repeatedly keeps hips, hands and feet visible. Camera movement tracks the fighters rather than replacing choreography, and cuts generally occur after a positional change or completed phrase.
- AI-video takeaways: For a 10–15 second homage, use three escalating phrases instead of one endless combo: clean technical exchange → harder re-entry with environmental displacement → fatigued final burst. Preserve a clear recovery pose between phases.
- Tags: the-raid-2, iko-uwais, silat, kitchen-fight, close-quarters, escalation, full-body, classic

- Direct watch — official demo: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2buBF742Pw

- Why it is worth studying: The current TGS build combines very fast gap-closing movement, sword attacks, wire traversal, a just-guard/parry mechanic called “流し,” and a slow-motion post-defeat slicing state. Those explicit state changes are highly reusable for AI action design.
- Action design: High-speed approach / wire entry → committed sword strike → timed parry / flow redirect → counter → enemy defeat → deliberate slow-motion slice state → kick / power-up / recovery choice → return to normal-speed movement.

- AI-video takeaways: Write normal-speed and slow-motion states as separate beats. A clean 15-second grammar is fast approach → one parry → one counter → explicit slow-motion finish → visible return to real-time movement. Avoid mixing wire movement, parry and slicing into the same instant.

## Anime

### Attack on Titan — Levi vs. Kenny Squad

- Category: Anime / 3D Spatial Pursuit / ODM Combat
- Watch: https://www.bilibili.tv/video/2043012140
- Why it is worth studying: A standout reference for combining character action with extreme camera translation through dense architecture. Levi’s movement is fast, but every burst is anchored to walls, rooftops, alleys or visible pursuers, so the audience can still understand where the next threat is coming from.
- Action design: Grapple / launch → obstacle skim → incoming-fire reaction → hard direction change → wall / roof anchor → re-acceleration → close pass / counter → immediate escape line.
- Camera / editing: Aggressive tracking, whip reorientation and perspective changes are motivated by Levi’s anchors and enemy fire. The sequence repeatedly gives the viewer a new spatial reference before the next acceleration.
- AI-video takeaways: For high-speed aerial pursuit, define 3–4 fixed environmental anchors and a clear destination for every burst. Use whip pans only when threat direction changes; do not let the camera orbit independently of the character trajectory.
- Tags: attack-on-titan, levi, kenny, odm, aerial-pursuit, whip-pan, spatial-anchor, anime-action
