## martial arts

### UFC Fight Night 2026 — Montel Jackson vs. Ricky Simon
- Type: live MMA / bantamweight striking
- Official highlight: https://www.ufc.com/video/160518
- Event page: https://www.ufc.com/event/ufc-fight-night-september-26-2026
- Date: 26 Sep 2026
- Why study it: useful real-contact reference for how a fight can stay measured for long stretches and then collapse into a very short decisive burst.
- Action design: probe/read at range → opponent steps into commitment → short counter sequence → visible loss of balance → finish. The setup matters as much as the knockout.
- Camera / editing: broadcast framing keeps both fighters, the cage edge and stance distance visible before tightening attention around the finish.
- AI-video takeaway: do not script every second as offense. Use a low-activity read phase, one clear trigger, then a 1–2 beat finishing burst with a concrete end state.
- Tags: mma, ufc, counter, range-control, knockout, realistic-combat, 2026

## film-TV

### Mission: Impossible – Fallout — Bathroom Fight
- Type: live-action close-quarters / three-person fight
- Official clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sLiZeg6zU4M
- Why study it: one of the cleanest references for a cramped 2-vs-1 fight where hard surfaces, mirrors, stalls and sinks constrain every movement.
- Action design: two attackers alternate initiative instead of attacking simultaneously; grabs, throws and short strikes repeatedly change who is closest to the target.
- Camera / editing: medium and medium-wide coverage preserves the three-person geometry; cuts usually follow a positional change or impact rather than hiding choreography.
- AI-video takeaway: for a three-person bathroom fight, define one active attacker, one recovering/queued attacker, and one environmental collision per beat. Keep the room layout fixed.
- Tags: mission-impossible, bathroom-fight, close-quarters, three-person, environmental-action, threat-handoff

### Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon — Jen vs. Shu Lien
- Type: live-action wuxia / multi-weapon duel
- Licensed clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vagbHi-vA-M
- Why study it: a classic example of making weapon changes readable. Shu Lien cycles through multiple weapons while Jen keeps the Green Destiny sword, so each exchange has a fresh range and silhouette.
- Action design: weapon mismatch → short test exchange → weapon failure/disarm → immediate switch → re-entry. The duel escalates by changing tools, not by simply increasing speed.
- Camera / editing: lateral and medium-wide framing preserves full weapon arcs and keeps the fighters’ screen relationship stable despite frequent weapon transitions.
- AI-video takeaway: treat every weapon change as an explicit state transition: old weapon exits frame → new weapon is acquired → stance/range changes → next exchange begins. Never morph weapons mid-swing.
- Tags: crouching-tiger-hidden-dragon, wuxia, swordfight, weapon-switch, range-change, yuen-woo-ping

## games

### Nioh 3 — Gameplay Showcase
- Type: game combat / samurai + ninja style switching
- Official video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GfPMuwk2Mzg
- Date: 22 Jan 2026
- Why study it: the combat visibly separates Samurai and Ninja movement grammars and allows switching between them during encounters.
- Action design: guarded samurai commitment → defensive read/deflect → explicit style switch → faster ninja reposition/attack → reset. The state change changes locomotion and attack rhythm, not just VFX.
- Camera / editing: stable third-person framing keeps the enemy as a spatial anchor while style changes alter the player character’s movement vocabulary.
- AI-video takeaway: if a character changes combat mode, also change stance, step length, attack range and recovery timing. Prompt the switch as a visible beat rather than an invisible attribute.
- Tags: nioh-3, samurai, ninja, style-switch, state-transition, game-combat, 2026

## stunt-choreography

### NIKAZE — Karate Street Fight
- Type: choreographed karate / street-fight short
- Watch: https://vimeo.com/80009640
- Credits: directed/choreographed by Andy Hourahine; stunt choreography by Blair Johannes.
- Why study it: a clean performer-focused reference built around karate movement rather than VFX or rapid editorial camouflage.
- Action design: measured stance and distance → linear entry → short hand/leg exchange → angle change → disengage/recovery. The short phrases make cause and response easy to isolate.
- Camera / editing: practical location coverage keeps performer silhouettes and attack lines visible, making the clip useful for extracting 2–5 second motion references.
- AI-video takeaway: good V2V source for grounded karate. Let the reference control body trajectory and timing; use the prompt only for character identity, costume, location and one camera treatment.
