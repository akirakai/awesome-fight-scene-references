# Fight Scene Reference Batch — 2026-09-10

A curated batch of non-duplicate fight references selected for AI video generation. The emphasis is on readable choreography, useful camera logic, explicit attack/response timing, and sequences that can be decomposed into short promptable action beats.

## Martial Arts

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Du Hongjie Men's Nanquan Gold Medal Performance

- Category: Martial Arts / Nanquan / Taolu
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t1iWJMBcUQE
- Why it is worth studying: Official WUSHU TV competition footage gives a clean, unobstructed view of explosive Southern-style body mechanics without cinematic editing hiding the transitions.
- Action design: Deep stance setup → explosive hand combination → sharp pivot / kick or direction change → low landing → deliberate recovery. The contrast between held positions and sudden acceleration makes the rhythm especially readable.
- Camera / editing: Neutral full-body tournament framing keeps feet, hips, torso and hand paths visible at the same time, making center-of-gravity shifts easy to study.
- AI-video takeaways: Excellent low-noise motion reference. Prompt the weight shift and stance first, then the strike path and recovery. Preserve brief held poses between explosive bursts instead of generating uninterrupted speed.
- Tags: nanquan, wushu, iwuf, taolu, explosive-movement, full-body-reference, stance, 2025

## Film / TV

### John Wick: Chapter 4 — Top-Down Shootout

- Category: Film / Gun-Fu / Overhead Action
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pksOSHCKp2k
- Alternate watch page: https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/john_wick_chapter_4/videos/8cgjCP6RcUyI
- Why it is worth studying: One of the clearest examples of a camera concept becoming the organizing rule of an action sequence: the overhead view turns rooms, walls, doors and enemy positions into a readable combat map.
- Action design: Advance through room → acquire one immediate threat → close-range shot / grapple solution → cross a doorway or wall boundary → acquire the next threat. The progression is spatial rather than a random stack of moves.
- Camera / editing: Sustained high-angle/top-down tracking preserves screen geography while the Dragon's Breath shotgun creates very clear visual punctuation at each impact. Architecture supplies natural transitions between micro-fights.
- AI-video takeaways: Strong template for a 10–15 second overhead action shot. Lock a single high camera path, keep the protagonist moving through 2–3 spatial cells, and allow only one active opponent/contact problem at a time. Use doors and walls as state transitions instead of cutting the camera arbitrarily.
- Tags: john-wick-4, top-down, gun-fu, overhead-camera, spatial-map, tracking, environmental-action

### Ip Man — Ip Man vs. Ten Black Belts

- Category: Film / Wing Chun / One-vs-Many
- Source: https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x8nkbc6
- Reference page: https://kungfukingdom.com/ip-man-10-man-fight-scene/
- Why it is worth studying: A classic one-versus-many sequence where speed comes from decisive target handoffs and compact Wing Chun mechanics rather than making every opponent attack simultaneously.
- Action design: Threat enters → center-line interception / chain-punch burst → short control, sweep or displacement → immediate orientation to the next opponent. Each attacker becomes a brief local problem before the fight advances.
- Camera / editing: Medium and wide coverage frequently keeps the current opponent, the next threat and Ip Man's body line readable; closer shots are reserved for impact emphasis rather than used to conceal choreography.
- AI-video takeaways: Useful one-vs-many prompt grammar: one active pair + one queued threat. Build 3–4 short attack-response modules and explicitly rotate the hero toward the next opponent after each finish. Do not prompt ten attackers to strike at the same time.
- Tags: ip-man, wing-chun, one-vs-many, centerline, target-handoff, compact-strikes, classic-action

## Anime

### Demon Slayer: Entertainment District Arc — Tengen Uzui vs. Gyutaro Climax

- Category: Anime / Dual-Weapon Duel / High-Speed Supernatural Combat
- Source: https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/GK9U35WDG/never-give-up
- Why it is worth studying: The climax balances extreme speed, dual weapons, debris and heavy effects while repeatedly returning to clear silhouettes and attack directions so the viewer can still understand who is winning each exchange.
- Action design: Clear guard / wind-up → explosive dash → paired weapon clash → displacement through the environment → brief orientation reset → stronger re-entry. The fight escalates through repeated clash-and-separate cycles instead of one continuous blur.
- Camera / editing: Rapid reframing and impact cuts are anchored on readable weapon lines and strong contact poses; effects bridge the fastest travel after the attacker/defender relationship has already been established.
- AI-video takeaways: For anime-style AI combat, define 4–6 anchor states rather than every micro-move: guard → approach → clash → knockback/recovery → re-entry → finishing attempt. Preserve attack direction across cuts and attach VFX to already-readable weapon motion.
- Tags: demon-slayer, tengen-uzui, gyutaro, dual-weapons, anime-action, anchor-pose, high-speed, clash

## Prompt patterns distilled from this batch

- **Explosive martial form:** held stance → weight shift → explosive strike phrase → direction change → low landing → deliberate recovery.
- **Overhead room-clear:** fixed high camera → enter spatial cell A → resolve one threat → cross doorway/wall boundary → resolve threat B → continue along one readable route.
- **One-vs-many:** active pair → short decisive exchange → next threat becomes active → hero reorients → next exchange; background opponents wait or reposition.
- **Character vocabulary:** define one range rule + one weapon rule + one explicit power/state change; do not let the character switch combat grammar accidentally.
- **Anime speed:** 4–6 anchor poses with effect/motion bridges between them; preserve attack direction and contact causality across cuts.
- **Reference-driven V2V:** reference clip controls choreography and contact timing; character refs control identity; scene refs control environment; prompt mainly controls cinematography and finish.
- **Long AI fight:** generate short compatible modules; carry forward end pose, facing, weapon state, target and camera side before starting the next module.
