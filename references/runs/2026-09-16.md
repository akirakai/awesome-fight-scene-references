# Fight Scene Reference Batch — 2026-09-16

Seven newly screened references for AI-video action design. This batch was checked against the repository for title-level duplication before writing. It mixes clean real-world combat, classic Hong Kong weapon/empty-hand choreography, a newly released game, recent hand-drawn anime action, stunt/action-design material, and a recent MiniMax H3 weapon-fight test.

## Martial Arts

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Sanda Men's 60kg Final

- Category: Martial Arts / Sanda / Live Combat
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHsPHibL0fA
- Source: WUSHU TV (IWUF WUSHU OFFICIAL)
- Published: 2025-12-09
- Why it is worth studying: Unlike a choreographed form, Sanda gives genuine attack-response timing, imperfect entries, defensive reactions, clinch pressure and resets. The competition framing keeps both fighters' stance, distance and ring position legible.
- Action design: probe / feint → committed punch-kick entry → defender check, evade or cover → possible catch / sweep / clinch → separation → stance reset. Misses and partial contacts are part of the rhythm.
- Camera / editing: stable broadcast-wide framing preserves both full bodies and the ring boundary, which makes it useful as a clean motion reference before adding cinematic camera movement.
- AI-video takeaways: for realistic martial-arts generation, explicitly include misses, blocks, weight recovery and brief re-measuring. Do not make both fighters throw continuously or make every strike land.
- Tags: sanda, live-combat, wushu, distance-management, attack-response, sweep, realistic-fighting

## Film / TV

### Fist of Legend — Chen Zhen vs. Fumio Funakoshi

- Category: Film / TV / Empty-Hand Duel / Classic Hong Kong Action
- Official excerpt: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ASM6eeAV6Ug
- Longer sequence reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xBW7r8feEp8
- Why it is worth studying: Yuen Woo-ping's choreography gives the two fighters visibly different tactical vocabularies while keeping the duel unusually grounded for 1990s Hong Kong action. The blindfold section is especially useful because it shifts the fight from visual reaction to balance, timing, sound and contact sensitivity.
- Action design: testing exchange → style adaptation → short punch/kick combinations → off-balancing / grappling → temporary reset → blindfolded contact-reading → counter. The scene evolves by changing the problem, not simply by making the moves faster.
- Camera / editing: medium and medium-wide coverage generally preserves the fighters' complete bodies; low angles and selective inserts intensify impact without hiding the underlying mechanics.
- AI-video takeaways: assign each fighter a distinct movement grammar and let the fight contain one explicit rules change. A 15-second version could move from visible technical exchange to eyes-obscured close-range contact without changing identities or screen direction.
- Tags: fist-of-legend, jet-li, yasuaki-kurata, yuen-woo-ping, blindfold-fight, style-contrast, classic

### Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon — Jen Yu vs. Yu Shu Lien Weapon-Room Duel

- Category: Film / TV / Weapon Duel / Wuxia
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a3sQIjbrMQw
- Why it is worth studying: One fighter keeps the Green Destiny sword while the other repeatedly changes weapons. That makes the sequence a near-perfect lesson in how a weapon change should alter range, timing and movement vocabulary instead of being a cosmetic prop swap.
- Action design: sword pressure → defender changes weapon → new range / guard rule → weapon breaks or is neutralized → immediate transition to another weapon → renewed exchange. Each weapon creates a different tactical micro-phase.
- Camera / editing: broad lateral and diagonal compositions preserve weapon arcs and distance; cuts tend to arrive around a weapon/state change, so the viewer rarely loses the duel's geometry.
- AI-video takeaways: when prompting weapon switching, specify a visible transition and a new movement grammar after each switch. Do not let a weapon morph mid-swing. For 15 seconds, limit the sequence to two weapon states plus one clear break/transition.
- Tags: crouching-tiger-hidden-dragon, michelle-yeoh, zhang-ziyi, wuxia, weapon-switching, swordfight, range-control

## Games

### Marvel's Wolverine — Launch Trailer / Combat Showcase

- Category: Games / Third-Person Melee / Aggressive Character Combat
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cfzG51AYco4
- Official game page: https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/marvels-wolverine/
- Launch date: 2026-09-15
- Why it is worth studying: The combat language is built around fast forward pressure, claw-specific close-range attacks, environmental impacts, ambushes and a Rage state. It is a useful current example of making one character's combat instantly recognizable through a small set of repeated verbs.
- Action design: pursuit / lunge → claw combination → enemy displacement or environmental contact → short reacquisition → technique or Rage escalation → decisive finisher. The protagonist remains the visual anchor even during violent multi-enemy moments.
- Camera / editing: third-person follow framing stays close to Logan's momentum and reframes when the target or tactical objective changes. Hero framing is aggressive but still keeps the attack vector readable.
- AI-video takeaways: define a character-specific combat vocabulary instead of generic 'fast fighting.' For a Wolverine-like sequence: forward lunge, compact claw arcs, body-driven impact, one wall/environment beat, then a single escalation state.
- Tags: marvels-wolverine, 2026, third-person-action, claws, rage-state, aggressive-movement, character-vocabulary

## Anime

### SEKIRO: NO DEFEAT — Wolf vs. Gyoubu Masataka Oniwa (Ashina Castle Gate)

- Category: Anime / Mounted Boss Fight / Sword vs. Spear
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCOub7E1-mA
- Source: KADOKAWAanime official
- Published: 2026-08-21; theatrical run began 2026-09-04
- Why it is worth studying: This is a recent fully hand-drawn 2D fight that solves a difficult scale mismatch: a grounded swordsman versus a mounted opponent with a long polearm. Gyoubu's horse and spear create large sweeping trajectories, while Wolf answers with compact deflection, evasive movement and the grappling hook.
- Action design: mounted charge / spear sweep → Wolf evades or deflects → horse carries the boss past → spatial reset → grappling-hook re-entry → close counter → immediate separation. The horse's momentum creates natural punctuation between exchanges.
- Camera / editing: wide shots establish scale and trajectory; closer cuts are reserved for deflection/contact beats. Directional continuity remains clear because the mounted attacker has a strong travel vector.
- AI-video takeaways: for human-vs-mounted or human-vs-large-target fights, make the large target's movement the spatial anchor. Use one charge vector, one defensive response and one re-entry mechanic rather than continuous orbiting chaos.
- Tags: sekiro-no-defeat, gyoubu, mounted-combat, spear, sword, grappling-hook, hand-drawn-anime, 2026

## Stunt / Choreography

### Mortal Kombat 1 — Fatality Action Reel (LBP Stunts Chicago)

- Category: Stunt / Choreography / Game Action Design / Motion Capture
- Watch: https://vimeo.com/879678268
- Credits: stunt coordination and action design by Emmanuel Manzanares / LBP Stunts Chicago for Mortal Kombat 1.
- Why it is worth studying: The reel sits directly between physical stunt performance and highly exaggerated game animation. Fatal blows and fatalities still depend on readable setup, body orientation, partner timing and an unmistakable finishing silhouette before VFX and impossible anatomy are layered on top.
- Action design: recognizable setup pose → committed capture / strike → one dominant direction of force → reaction / body displacement → finishing pose or state. The most extreme moves remain readable because the physical intention is simple.
- Camera / editing: game-style presentation isolates decisive beats and lets a single pose or impact carry the moment rather than cutting through every micro-action.
- AI-video takeaways: when generating exaggerated action, start from a physically legible stunt phrase and reserve the impossible element for one beat. This is a strong reference for converting mocap/previs into stylized AI action without losing cause-and-effect.
- Tags: mortal-kombat-1, lbp-stunts, emmanuel-manzanares, action-design, motion-capture, fatality, exaggerated-action

## AI Action

### MiniMax H3 — Fast Cold-Weapon Duel / Multi-Weapon Test

- Category: AI Action / MiniMax H3 / Weapon Fight / Workflow Test
- Watch: https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV11cb46tEoc/
- Published: 2026-09-06
- Workflow references noted by creator: MiniMax H3 Timeline Director plus latent upscaling / secondary sampling.
- Why it is worth studying: This test focuses specifically on rapid cold-weapon exchanges and weapon variety, making it more diagnostic than a general cinematic demo. It is useful for checking whether H3 preserves weapon identity, hand-to-weapon attachment and opponent relationship during fast action.
- Evaluation focus: weapon shape stability, grip consistency, attack-response causality, contact timing, screen direction and temporal continuity. The workflow also separates base motion generation from later upscaling / secondary sampling.
- Camera / editing: the useful question is whether rapid motion remains readable without relying on excessive blur or camera shake. Weapon attacks should retain a clear before/contact/after state.
- AI-video takeaways: for H3 weapon fights, write the prompt as timed action phrases and score motion before image polish: guard → attack → parry/contact → recoil → reposition → second attack. Treat upscaling as a finishing stage, not a substitute for correct choreography.
- Tags: minimax-h3, ai-action, cold-weapons, weapon-duel, timeline-director, motion-continuity, ref2v, 2026
