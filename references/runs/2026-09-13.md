## Martial Arts

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Xu Wu Women's Taijiquan Gold Medal Performance

- Category: Martial Arts / Performance / Taijiquan
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t2oHYVoVCcI
- Why it is worth studying: An IWUF/WUSHU TV official competition performance with a clean, stable view of the full body. Taijiquan is especially useful for AI motion study because balance, weight transfer, torso rotation, hand paths, and recovery are all exposed rather than hidden by fast cuts.
- Action design: Continuous weight transfer → controlled torso turn → coordinated arm path → low or extended stance → deliberate recovery. The useful lesson is that apparent softness still depends on precise center-of-mass control.
- Camera / editing: Stable competition coverage keeps the performer readable from head to foot and provides a low-noise reference for extracting movement without compensating for cinematic camera tricks.
- AI-video takeaways: Strong reference for elegant martial-arts characters, swordless wuxia setup beats, or slow-to-fast action transitions. Prompt the weight shift and torso direction first, then add hand motion, cloth dynamics, and camera movement.
- Tags: wushu, taijiquan, xu-wu, full-body, balance, weight-transfer, motion-reference

## Film / TV

### SPL: Kill Zone — Donnie Yen vs. Wu Jing Alley Duel

- Category: Film / TV / Baton-vs-Knife Duel
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkL-pE0clEk
- Curated context: https://asia-archive.si.edu/the-art-of-action-25-years-of-hong-kong-fight-scenes/
- Why it is worth studying: One of the clearest classic close-quarters weapon duels: baton reach, knife pressure, parries, hand checks, distance changes, and frustration escalation remain readable in a narrow alley. The sequence is also notable for containing substantial improvisation between highly trained performers.
- Action design: Measure distance → knife pressure → baton interception → rapid weapon-contact burst → angle change → re-entry → decisive control. The two weapons have different tactical ranges, so the choreography never feels like mirrored swordplay.
- Camera / editing: The alley creates a strong movement axis. Medium and medium-wide coverage keeps both weapon hands and most footwork visible, while cuts preserve screen direction instead of hiding contact.
- AI-video takeaways: Excellent template for short-weapon V2V. Lock weapon hand, reach hierarchy, contact timing, and camera side. Write each exchange as attack → interception → displacement → reset rather than "fast knife fight."
- Tags: spl, kill-zone, donnie-yen, wu-jing, baton, knife, close-quarters, weapon-duel

### Ong-Bak — Muay Thai Fight Clip / Fight-Club Vocabulary

- Category: Film / TV / Muay Thai
- Source: https://www.moviefone.com/movie/ong-bak-muay-thai-warrior/19891/videos/V73wghZr/
- Alternate direct clip: https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x9awz7y
- Why it is worth studying: Tony Jaa's movement vocabulary is unusually distinct: hard linear entries, elbows, knees, committed kicks, explosive jumps, and visibly completed recoveries. The scene is useful for building a character whose fighting style has a recognizable physical grammar instead of generic "martial arts."
- Action design: Read opponent → explosive entry → one committed Muay Thai strike → visible impact/displacement → brief stance recovery → next entry. Power comes from full-body commitment rather than long hand-speed combinations.
- Camera / editing: The framing generally gives kicks, knees, elbows, and landings enough space to read. The rhythm lets individual signature techniques finish before moving to the next beat.
- AI-video takeaways: Use one signature technique per beat. For a 10–15 second generation, choose 3–4 actions such as elbow entry → knee strike → round kick → jump-knee finish, with a clear recovery between them. Do not ask the model to improvise endless Muay Thai combinations.
- Tags: ong-bak, tony-jaa, muay-thai, signature-moves, full-body-impact, martial-arts-film

## Games

### Fable — Official Combat Gameplay | gamescom 2026

- Category: Games / Fantasy Action RPG / Style-Weaving Combat
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eEKbR70uSLc
- Official / publisher context: https://www.gematsu.com/2026/08/fable-gamescom-2026-combat-gameplay
- Published: 2026-08-28
- Why it is worth studying: A long combat-focused gameplay demonstration where melee, ranged attacks, and magic are intentionally designed to flow into one another. It is useful for AI fantasy combat because each mode has a clear visual function instead of becoming one undifferentiated VFX burst.
- Action design: Melee commitment → ranged or spell extension → enemy reaction/displacement → reposition → return to another combat mode. The Lantern Keeper boss material also shows how large enemy telegraphs can create readable punish windows.
- Camera / editing: Third-person gameplay keeps the hero, current target, and incoming threats legible over sustained combat instead of relying on montage.
- AI-video takeaways: Treat weapon, ranged, and magic actions as explicit states. A practical 15-second structure is melee starter → spell extension → enemy knockback → camera/hero reposition → heavy finishing action. Give each state a clear start and end pose.
- Tags: fable, gamescom-2026, fantasy-combat, melee, magic, ranged, boss-fight, state-machine

## Anime

### SEKIRO: NO DEFEAT — Wolf vs. Genichiro, “Battle in the Susuki Field”

- Category: Anime / Hand-Drawn 2D / Sword Duel
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1g4pIx2Pjjw
- Release context: https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2026/09/03/171693.html
- Published: 2026-09-03
- Why it is worth studying: A recent fully hand-drawn 2D action clip centered on Wolf and Genichiro. It translates Sekiro's parry-heavy combat into animation while preserving sword intent, pauses, directional attacks, clash timing, and character hierarchy.
- Action design: Guard/read → committed cut → deflection/clash → short displacement → reorientation → renewed attack. The exchange is especially useful for understanding how to make parries feel causal rather than decorative sparks.
- Camera / editing: Dynamic cuts and close approaches are anchored by readable blade silhouettes and strong screen direction. The scene lets impacts and brief pauses punctuate the speed instead of maintaining constant blur.
- AI-video takeaways: For anime sword combat, lock 4–6 anchor states and specify which blade initiates each clash. Let smear, sparks, grass motion, and camera acceleration connect those anchors. Do not prompt both fighters to attack continuously at the same time.
- Tags: sekiro-no-defeat, wolf, genichiro, anime, swordfight, parry, hand-drawn, 2026

## Stunt / Choreography

### Melissa Lischer — Stunt Reel: Action Sequence

- Category: Stunt / Choreography / Close-Combat Test Sequence
- Source: https://vimeo.com/1107046030
- Why it is worth studying: A compact fight-focused reel explicitly built around close-combat choreography, dynamic kicks, reversals, and screen performance. Compared with finished feature-film scenes, it carries less narrative and VFX noise, so body trajectory and partner timing are easier to isolate.
- Action design: Entry → close-range exchange → dynamic kick → reversal/counter → recovery. The reversals are especially useful for AI because they create clear moments when active-attacker ownership changes.
- Camera / editing: The visual language is inspired by modern action cinema while still keeping the performer as the center of attention. Shots are short enough to study as reusable 2–5 second action phrases.
- AI-video takeaways: Good source for direct motion-reference clipping. Preserve attacker/defender timing, contact point, body orientation, and final recovery pose; let the character reference and environment reference handle appearance and setting instead of rewriting the choreography in text.

## AI Action

### Seedance 2.5 — Wuxia Fight Test Clip

- Category: AI Action / Seedance 2.5 / Character-Reference Fight
- Source: https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1urM96WEN7/
- Published: 2026-08-03
- Why it is worth studying: The creator reports generating the wuxia fight primarily from character images without unusually complex prompts, making it a useful real-world test of how much action structure Seedance 2.5 can infer while maintaining referenced people.
- Action design: The useful benchmark is not just whether the motion looks fast, but whether attack/response causality, full-body trajectory, target order, and recovery remain coherent across wider shots.
- Camera / editing: The creator specifically notes that close-ups can look strong while wider action still shows blur/dragging at 720p. That makes the clip useful for deciding when to move a fight closer to camera and when to simplify choreography.
- AI-video takeaways: Use wide shots for fewer, larger actions and reserve dense hand/face detail for closer framings. When evaluating a generation, score wide-shot body clarity separately from close-up identity quality; do not let a beautiful close-up hide broken full-body motion.
- Tags: seedance-2-5, ai-action, wuxia, character-reference, wide-shot, motion-blur, identity-consistency
