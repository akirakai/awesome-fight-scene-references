## Martial Arts / Performance

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Nangun

- Category: Martial Arts / Southern staff
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nEDISB3fHDw
- Source: WUSHU TV (IWUF WUSHU OFFICIAL), 2025 World Wushu Championships.
- Why it is worth studying: Competition coverage exposes the whole kinetic chain without film editing hiding the mechanics.
- Action design: Deep southern stances, sudden stance drops, explosive direction changes, staff sweeps, thrusts and power accents. The weapon path is visibly driven by legs, hips and torso rather than detached arm motion.
- Camera / editing: Stable full-body sports framing keeps feet, center of gravity and both ends of the staff readable.
- AI-video takeaways: Excellent motion reference for staff-wielding characters. Prompt from the body outward: stance load → hip/torso rotation → staff arc → foot reposition → recovery. Add costume motion and cinematic camera movement only after the weapon trajectory is stable.
- Tags: nangun, staff, wushu, iwuf, full-body-reference, weapon-trajectory, body-mechanics

## Film / TV

### The Raid: Redemption — Three-Way Battle to the Death

- Category: Film / Silat / 2v1
- Watch: https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_raid_redemption/videos/4NCR1fgiTZ2L
- Source: Rotten Tomatoes official clip hub for *The Raid: Redemption*.
- Why it is worth studying: One of the clearest templates for making a 2v1 fight readable instead of chaotic.
- Action design: Rama and Andi rarely attack as an undifferentiated pair. The sequence repeatedly creates an active attacker/defender relationship, then uses the second ally as an interrupter or momentum changer. Mad Dog's pressure, fatigue and recovery remain visible.
- Camera / editing: Medium and wide framings preserve all three bodies and the current line of attack; longer exchanges are allowed to play before the tactical relationship changes.
- AI-video takeaways: For a 15-second 2v1 scene, define only one active pair at a time: A attacks C → B interrupts once → C redirects to B → A re-enters → decisive combined beat. Keep failed attacks and recovery steps so the choreography has weight.
- Tags: the-raid, silat, 2v1, active-pair, interrupter, readable-action, fatigue

## Games

### Onimusha: Way of the Sword — Severing Fates Trailer

- Category: Games / Sword combat / Boss fight
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4zK4Yddz_8o
- Source: Capcom, trailer published August 2026.
- Why it is worth studying: The Rasho-gan encounter demonstrates how to stage a human swordsman against a much larger multi-armed enemy without losing the attack line.
- Action design: The boss telegraphs broad multi-limb attacks while Musashi answers with compact evasions, parries and counters. Large enemy motion creates the primary rhythm; the hero's movement is shorter and more precise.
- Camera / editing: Third-person framing keeps the boss silhouette, attack origin and player response in the same visual field, then pushes closer for high-value contact beats.
- AI-video takeaways: Strong asymmetric-fight template: boss anticipation → large committed swing → hero short evade/parry → visible contact window → one counter → separation. Do not make every boss limb attack independently at the same time.
- Tags: onimusha, boss-fight, sword, parry, asymmetric-combat, multi-arm, 2026

### Sifu — Fight Club Gameplay Teaser

- Category: Games / Kung fu / One-vs-many
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vy-enf5xxgQ
- Source: PlayStation official channel.
- Why it is worth studying: The game turns one-vs-many kung fu into short tactical problems with strong spatial clarity and heavy use of environment.
- Action design: Short punch/kick chains, directional defense, quick target changes, wall/table interactions and improvised objects are linked by repositioning rather than endless combos.
- Camera / editing: The third-person camera keeps the player and immediate threat readable while letting background enemies wait their turn or reposition.
- AI-video takeaways: For corridor/club fights, lock the protagonist and define target handoffs explicitly: enemy A enters → 2–3 beat exchange → environmental contact → pivot to B → short counter → exit/reposition. Background enemies should threaten, not all attack simultaneously.
- Tags: sifu, kung-fu, one-vs-many, environment, target-handoff, game-camera, spatial-clarity

- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S1tF4wXoiKc
- Source: official Gintama YouTube channel, published January 29, 2026.
- Why it is worth studying: A compact battle PV that separates two allied movement vocabularies while escalating toward Kagura's power release.
- Action design: Shinpachi supplies blade-based interventions while Kagura brings more explosive close-range physicality; Abuto remains the central threat rather than becoming visually lost between them.

## AI Action

- Category: AI Action / 12-second multi-shot fight
- Watch: https://vimeo.com/1181844347
- Source: Shaik YAsin, recently indexed Vimeo AI-action experiment.
- Why it is worth studying: A compact AI-generated action micro-sequence explicitly designed around multi-angle coverage, character consistency, antagonist staging and trailer-level pacing.

## Reusable prompting patterns from this batch

1. **2v1 readable fight:** active pair → one interrupter → role reversal → re-entry → combined finish.
2. **Large boss vs. human:** large telegraphed attack → short evade/parry → clear contact window → single counter → separation.
3. **Weapon form:** center-of-gravity shift → hip/torso rotation → weapon arc → foot recovery → next phrase.
4. **One-vs-many:** immediate threat A → short exchange → environmental interaction → motivated pivot → threat B.
5. **Reference-video workflow:** reference video owns choreography; character reference owns identity; environment reference owns set; prompt owns continuity, style and constraints.
