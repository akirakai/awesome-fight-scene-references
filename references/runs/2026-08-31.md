## Games

- Verification: https://www.gematsu.com/2026/08/fable-gamescom-2026-combat-gameplay
- Type: Game combat / melee + ranged + magic
- Why study it: a current 16-minute combat showcase built around seamless transitions between three clearly different combat states instead of one endlessly mutating moveset.
- Action design: close-range melee establishes pressure, ranged attacks change distance, and magic creates a visible tactical/state transition.

- 15s pattern: 0–4s melee close → 4–8s explicit magic transition → 8–12s ranged reposition → 12–15s decisive combined finish.

## Martial Arts / Performance

- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2pzgcPcnmtc
- Type: Real martial arts / weapon forms
- Why study it: official IWUF/WUSHU TV competition footage is exceptionally useful for extracting authentic spear/staff mechanics without cinematic cutting hiding the body.
- Action design: clear stance changes, rear-hand drive, front-hand guidance, torso rotation, shaft arcs, leaps and recovery positions reveal where weapon speed actually comes from.

## AI Action

### Incantation — Natural Language as the Action Interface for Multi-Entity Video World Models
- Project / evidence videos: https://matrixteam-ai.github.io/pages/Incanation/
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18601
- Type: AI action / interactive multi-entity combat generation
- Why study it: one of the most directly applicable 2026 research references for AI fight prompting. It controls separate entities with parallel natural-language action slots at 0.25-second latent-frame granularity and demonstrates combat in Elden Ring and King of Fighters worlds.
- Action design: every participant receives its own short action instruction instead of relying on a single global caption. This exposes attack initiation, response, simultaneous actions and role separation explicitly.
- Camera / editing: one shared camera stream makes it easy to judge whether identity binding and action causality survive while multiple actors receive different controls.
- AI-video takeaway: build a 15-second fight as parallel timelines, not one sentence saying “they fight.” For each beat specify Fighter A action + Fighter B response, then update both from the resulting state. A third participant should enter through its own short slot rather than becoming background chaos.
- 15s pattern: A closes/B guards → A commits/B evades → B counters/A staggers → both recover/reposition → new paired action → final committed exchange.
- Tags: incantation, ai-action, multi-entity, natural-language-control, per-entity-prompts, 0-25s-control, combat-generation, 2026

## Reusable prompting lessons from this batch

1. **One movement grammar per fighter.** Large rider, agile shinobi, grappler, spear performer and magic user should not share the same locomotion vocabulary.
2. **Make state changes explicit.** Weapon swaps, magic casts, grapples and traversal tools need a readable transition frame.
3. **Write paired causality.** Attacker commits → defender responds → contact/avoidance changes the state → both recover from that new state.
4. **Keep the camera simpler as choreography gets harder.** Stable side/axis and full-body readability outperform arbitrary cinematic motion during complex exchanges.
5. **For 15 seconds, use 3–5 meaningful beats.** More moves usually reduce identity, contact and weapon consistency rather than increasing excitement.
