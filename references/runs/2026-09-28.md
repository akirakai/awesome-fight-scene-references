## martial arts

### Asian Games 2026 — Adeola Fay Robert vs. Fatemehzahra Saeidabadi, Women's Kumite -55kg Final

- **Type:** elite karate kumite / live competition
- **Source:** https://www.mewatch.sg/asiangames/karate
- **Context:** Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, 22 Sep 2026; Adeola Fay Robert won 6-4.
- **Why study it:** Excellent reference for realistic stop-start fighting rhythm rather than endless combinations.
- **Action design:** probe/feint → explosive entry → scoring attempt or partial exchange → immediate disengage → guard reset.
- **Camera / editing:** broadcast wide/medium coverage preserves both fighters, footwork, distance and boundary lines.
- **AI-video takeaways:** explicitly prompt separation and reset beats. Keep one initiator and one responder per beat; avoid making both fighters attack continuously.
- **Tags:** karate, kumite, sparring, spacing, feint, burst, reset

## film-TV

### John Wick: Chapter 4 — Osaka / Nunchaku & Caine Action Reference

- **Type:** live-action weapon choreography
- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZHJnDiLRpYE
- **Why study it:** Strong example of readable weapon exchanges inside dense production design, with different weapon vocabularies remaining distinct.
- **Action design:** threat read → weapon interception → short counter → positional shift → re-engagement; nunchaku beats are separated by visible recoil and recovery.
- **Camera / editing:** composed medium/wide coverage, lateral tracking and motivated reframing rather than cutting on every hit.
- **AI-video takeaways:** keep the weapon state explicit across every beat; define which hand holds the weapon, the opponent reaction, and the end pose before adding camera motion.
- **Tags:** nunchaku, close-quarters, weapon-fight, tracking, Donnie-Yen, John-Wick

### Chocolate (2008) — Warehouse Fight

- **Type:** live-action martial-arts / environmental fight
- **Source:** https://vimeo.com/36667149
- **Why study it:** Great reference for acrobatic movement using shelves, walls and narrow warehouse geometry as choreography anchors.
- **Action design:** approach → obstacle-assisted evade/strike → landing/recovery → next spatial station.
- **Camera / editing:** wider framings make takeoff, airborne trajectory and landing readable; environment establishes clear movement lanes.
- **AI-video takeaways:** define 2-3 fixed environment landmarks and let the character travel between them. Treat every jump as takeoff → airborne action → landing → balance recovery.
- **Tags:** warehouse, acrobatics, muay-thai, environmental-fight, full-body, stunt

## games

### Onimusha: Way of the Sword — Ken no Michi Shorts / Combat Reference

- **Type:** game combat / samurai swordplay
- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JVMOpsqIFPg&list=PLRyFvANEV5hA
- **Why study it:** Recent 2026 reference built around blade-on-blade timing, Issen-style precision counters and clear stance changes.
- **Action design:** enemy telegraph → guard/read → clash or deflect → committed counter → soul/ability state → return to neutral.
- **Camera / editing:** third-person combat camera keeps attacker, target and sword contact readable while allowing strong impact framing.
- **AI-video takeaways:** do not describe “fast sword fighting” generically. Prompt the telegraph, exact clash, recoil direction and post-hit stance as separate states.
- **Tags:** samurai, sword, parry, counter, game-combat, state-machine

## anime

### Blue Eye Samurai — “Cliff Showdown” Official Clip

- **Type:** animated sword fight / outnumbered combat
- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCVl5LR8A5o
- **Why study it:** Clear multi-opponent staging: Mizu rarely fights every enemy simultaneously; threats enter in readable waves.
- **Action design:** primary attacker enters → Mizu answers → attacker exits/displaces → next threat takes priority → short reset.
- **Camera / editing:** bold perspective changes and dynamic framing are still anchored to the cliff edge, enemy formation and Mizu's facing.
- **AI-video takeaways:** for one-vs-many, enforce **one active attacker + one queued threat**. Preserve screen direction and a fixed environment edge as a spatial anchor.
- **Tags:** animation, swordfight, one-vs-many, threat-handoff, perspective, silhouette

## stunt-choreography

### BRAWLER — Martial Arts Action Short Film (4K)

- **Type:** action short / fight choreography showcase
- **Source:** https://vimeo.com/949671007
- **Credits:** Action Director/Coordinator Lukaz Leong; Fight Choreography Lukaz Leong & Isky Fay.
- **Why study it:** Award-winning choreography with clean physical phrasing and cinematic coverage, useful as a bridge between stunt-vis and finished-film language.
- **Action design:** short exchange → positional consequence → separation → new entry, instead of an uninterrupted combo.
- **Camera / editing:** coverage emphasizes contact and body trajectory; cuts tend to follow tactical changes rather than hide every technique.
- **AI-video takeaways:** extract 2-5 second phrases for V2V. Let the reference drive trajectory/contact while prompts only add identity, wardrobe, location and one camera instruction.
- **Tags:** stunt, choreography, action-short, hand-to-hand, V2V-reference

### Seedance 2.5 — “理解能力 / 打斗全进化” Same-Prompt Fight Tests

- **Type:** AI-action benchmark / generation comparison
- **Source:** https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1AyGg6TEZH/
- **Date:** 1 Aug 2026
- **Why study it:** Large-scale same-prompt/reference testing makes it useful for separating choreography quality from prompt luck.
- **Action design:** compare whether the model preserves initiator/responder order, contact, body trajectory and post-impact recovery across repeated generations.
- **Camera / editing:** evaluate whether dynamic camera motion supports the action or merely hides anatomy/contact errors.
- **AI-video takeaways:** score outputs in this order: identity → trajectory → attack/response timing → contact → recovery → camera → VFX. A fight is not “stable” if only the camera and effects look good.
- **Tags:** Seedance-2.5, AI-fight, benchmark, prompt-adherence, motion-reference
