## Martial Arts

### ONE Friday Fights 170 — Pompet vs. Yodlekpet III

- Category: Martial Arts / Muay Thai / Real Competition
- Source / highlights: https://www.onefc.com/news/the-inner-circle-30-and-one-friday-fights-170-results-and-highlights-from-every-match/
- Main-event recap: https://www.onefc.com/news/one-friday-fights-170-main-event-result-pompet-edges-yodlekpet-in-thrilling-firefight-to-settle-trilogy/
- Event date: 2026-09-11
- Why it is worth studying: Fresh elite Muay Thai footage gives a reality check against over-choreographed AI fighting. Pompet and Yodlekpet spend meaningful time probing, reading, defending and resetting, so the decisive exchanges feel earned rather than mechanically continuous.
- Action design: Jab/probe → body-kick lane opens → opponent pressures or checks → short committed exchange → guard/footwork reset. Real misses, partial contacts and defensive recovery are part of the rhythm.
- Camera / editing: Neutral broadcast coverage keeps both fighters, stance width, range and the contact line readable for most exchanges.
- AI-video takeaways: For grounded striking, do not prompt nonstop successful hits. Use short causal phrases such as probe → committed strike → block/check/partial contact → stance recovery → new entry. Preserve guard reset and foot repositioning after every burst.
- Tags: muay-thai, one-friday-fights-170, pompet, yodlekpet, real-combat, guard-reset, distance-management, 2026

### 17th World Wushu Championships — Han Xueshi Women's Gunshu Gold Medal Performance

- Category: Martial Arts / Performance / Gunshu
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nBaGM4OzDS8
- Official result context: https://www.iwuf.org/xhimg/soft/250923/16-250923212437.pdf
- Why it is worth studying: WUSHU TV's official competition footage exposes the entire long-weapon movement chain with almost no cinematic noise: foot placement, center-of-gravity shifts, shoulder/hip turns, two-hand staff control, leaps and recovery are all visible.
- Action design: Weight shift → rear-hand drive / front-hand guidance → long staff arc or spin → direction change → leap or level change → controlled landing and recovery. The staff should read as one rigid object with a continuous axis, not a flexible visual effect.
- Camera / editing: Stable full-body competition framing makes this especially useful for extracting clean 2–5 second motion-reference phrases.
- AI-video takeaways: Lock the hands-to-staff relationship, staff axis, foot placement and landing first. Add costume motion, hair, wind and cinematic camera movement only after the weapon trajectory is stable.
- Tags: wushu, gunshu, staff, han-xueshi, iwuf, long-weapon, full-body, motion-reference

## Film / TV

### Raging Fire — Donnie Yen Backroom Brawl

- Category: Film / TV / Close-Quarters Multi-Attacker Brawl
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GO3ZTtXPpR0
- Official film page: https://www.wellgousa.com/films/raging-fire
- Why it is worth studying: This official Well Go USA clip is a compact example of Hong Kong-style close-quarters staging where a strong lead remains readable while several nearby threats compete for attention.
- Action design: Immediate threat enters → compact strike/control exchange → opponent is displaced → protagonist reorients to the next threat → another short exchange. The useful structure is target handoff, not simultaneous crowd chaos.
- Camera / editing: Tight backroom geometry naturally limits movement options and gives the camera strong walls, tables and bodies as spatial anchors. Coverage stays close enough for impact without turning every hit into an isolated insert.
- AI-video takeaways: For one-vs-many V2V, keep one active attacker plus one queued threat. Preserve protagonist orientation and screen direction between handoffs, and let the room constrain the choreography instead of adding more moves.
- Tags: raging-fire, donnie-yen, benny-chan, one-vs-many, close-quarters, target-handoff, hong-kong-action

## Games

### NINJA GAIDEN 4 — Yakumo Bloodraven Form Combat

- Category: Games / High-Speed Sword Action / Combat State Switching
- Source / official combat breakdown: https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/06/08/ninja-gaiden-4-prevew-interview-xbox-games-showcase/
- Official game page: https://www.xbox.com/en-US/games/ninja-gaiden-4
- Why it is worth studying: Yakumo's Base Form and Bloodraven Form deliberately use different combat rules. The source explains that Bloodraven changes regular inputs, can break guards, enables a Hōgeki counter against powerful attacks, and creates different risk/reward choices at long and close range.
- Action design: Base-form pressure/read → explicit Bloodraven activation → guard-break or timed Hōgeki counter → stagger/punish window → disengage or finisher → return to neutral. Form switching changes the movement vocabulary rather than merely recoloring the same combo.
- Camera / editing: A consistent third-person gameplay camera makes the state change legible through stance, weapon trajectory, speed and enemy reaction instead of relying on cinematic cuts.
- AI-video takeaways: Treat supernatural or powered combat as a state machine. Show the transition frame clearly, assign a different tactical rule to each state, and reserve the biggest speed/VFX burst for the state change or finisher.
- Tags: ninja-gaiden-4, yakumo, bloodraven, sword-combat, state-switch, counter, guard-break, game-action

## Anime

### Naruto Shippuden Episode 375 — Kakashi vs. Obito

- Category: Anime / Hand-to-Hand Duel / Match-Cut Choreography
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gi8jxlEiQ50
- Official episode: https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/GYNQ1PX2Y/kakashi-vs-obito
- Choreography analysis: https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/features/2020/8/7/feature-how-kakashi-vs-obito-exemplified-one-of-naruto-shippudens-greatest-strengths
- Why it is worth studying: The duel combines unusually grounded hand-to-hand choreography with emotionally motivated flashback match cuts. Crunchyroll's analysis highlights the fluid but controlled character animation, tight pacing, reversals and the way childhood flashbacks are woven directly into the brawl.
- Action design: Committed attack → block/redirect → short counter → positional reset → renewed exchange. The flashback version often mirrors a present-day silhouette or motion, making memory itself part of the choreography.
- Camera / editing: Hands, torsos and attack direction remain readable during fast exchanges. Match cuts bridge past and present by preserving pose, direction or movement intent rather than cutting randomly for speed.
- AI-video takeaways: Excellent template for a duel with temporal echoes. Lock 3–5 present-day anchor exchanges; if adding a memory cut, reuse the same silhouette, camera side and action vector, then return to the present on a matching pose.
- Tags: naruto-shippuden, kakashi, obito, hand-to-hand, match-cut, flashback, anime-choreography, duel

## Stunt / Choreography

### Yo Soy Un Hombre Loco — Final Duel

- Category: Stunt / Choreography / Two-Person Fight Short
- Source: https://vimeo.com/47220275
- Full short: https://vimeo.com/25227008
- Why it is worth studying: LBP Stunts Chicago published this fight excerpt specifically as the final duel from an award-winning action short. The Vimeo page credits fight choreography to Vladislav Rimburg and Emmanuel Manzanares and notes wins for Best Fight Choreography and Best Martial Arts Action Sequence at the 2011 Action on Film festival.
- Action design: Clear entry → committed attack → defensive response → reversal/counter → separation/recovery. Because the piece is built around performers and choreography rather than VFX spectacle, attack ownership and partner timing are easy to isolate.
- Camera / editing: Framing is designed to showcase the physical exchange; cuts support changes in angle or tactical state rather than hiding incomplete movement.
- AI-video takeaways: Strong low-noise source for direct motion-reference clipping. Harvest 2–5 second phrases and preserve attacker/defender roles, contact timing, camera side and final recovery pose. Let character/scene references replace appearance without rewriting the physical sequence.
- Tags: stunt-choreography, lbp-stunts, vlad-rimburg, emmanuel-manzanares, fight-short, duel, motion-reference

## AI Action

### Combat Making — MiniMax H3 High-Density Fight Workflow

- Category: AI Action / MiniMax H3 / ComfyUI / Local Fight Generation
- Watch / translated workflow: https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1xnYt6uEhy/
- Original video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uhG307gPHco
- Published: 2026-09-10
- Why it is worth studying: A very recent creator workflow focused specifically on generating dense martial-arts fight scenes locally with MiniMax H3. The demonstration compares two routes: a stack of specialized LoRAs for action vocabulary, impact physics and motion-continuity repair, versus a fully fine-tuned Singularity diffusion model supporting multiple generation modes.
- Action design: The useful diagnostic is whether high-density motion still preserves attacker/defender causality, foot and center trajectory, visible contact, recoil and a recoverable end state instead of collapsing into fast but unrelated body motion.
- Camera / editing: The workflow is valuable because motion quality can be judged before heavy post-production. High camera energy should be treated as a separate variable rather than a way to hide broken interactions.
- AI-video takeaways: Evaluate fight generations on four independent axes: paired action/reaction, body trajectory, contact/impact physics, and temporal continuity. For long fights, solve a short action phrase first, then extend from a clean recovery state; compare the same prompt/reference across base, LoRA-stack and fine-tuned variants.
