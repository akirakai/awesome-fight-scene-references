# Stunt & Choreography References

## The Raid 2 — Choreography Behind the Scenes
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qXecmLI12GY
- Study: direct behind-the-scenes material showing how intricate Pencak Silat screen choreography is rehearsed and staged.
- Movement design: continuous attack/counter logic, tight hand fighting, progressive escalation and clear transitions between empty-hand and weapon work.
- Camera / editing: choreography is designed first as a continuous physical flow, then broken into shots while preserving screen direction and exhaustion progression.
- AI-video takeaway: build fight prompts as a choreography arc: technical exchange → escalating pressure → fatigue → rough desperate finish. This produces more believable progression than stacking random spectacular moves.
- Tags: pencak-silat, rehearsal, choreography, behind-the-scenes, grounded-action

## Creed — Single-Take Boxing Fight
- Source: https://nofilmschool.com/2015/12/anatomy-scene-ryan-coogler-narrates-single-take-fight-scene-creed
- Study: a boxing match staged around the performer and Steadicam operator as one synchronized choreography system.
- Movement design: boxing combinations, clinches, knockdowns and corner transitions are timed around camera position rather than treated as independent action.
- Camera / editing: extended uninterrupted Steadicam movement changes angle and distance inside the ring while maintaining spatial clarity.
- AI-video takeaway: for a believable AI oner, choreograph the camera as another participant: orbit, cross behind a fighter, settle into profile for an exchange, then push closer after a decisive hit.
- Tags: boxing, oner, steadicam, camera-choreography, spatial-continuity

## Kingsman: The Secret Service — Church Fight BTS
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G9sy_qwhjm8
- Study: behind-the-scenes look at a highly stylized fight designed to feel like a continuous shot despite complex staging and transitions.
- Movement design: rapid target switching, environmental interaction and rhythmic chains of short action ideas.
- Camera / editing: aggressive camera travel, whip movement and concealed transitions maintain apparent continuity while constantly changing composition.
- AI-video takeaway: useful for hyper-kinetic sequences where the camera should connect several micro-fights without losing the protagonist. Prompt a clear subject lock plus motivated whip transitions between nearby threats.

## The Raid 2 — Kitchen Fight Design
- Source: https://dailydead.com/exclusive-interview-raid-2s-iko-uwais/
- Study: Iko Uwais describes roughly six weeks of preparation and a choreography that evolves from hand-to-hand exchanges into small-knife combat.
- Movement design: the scene has an explicit progression rather than a flat stream of moves; technical exchanges gradually become more desperate and damaging.
- Camera / editing: short shot pieces still preserve a continuous sense of physical exhaustion and escalating stakes.
- AI-video takeaway: define phases in longer generations: controlled parry/counter exchange → weapon introduction → fatigue → brutal final beat. Keep each phase visually distinct.
- Tags: pencak-silat, knife-fight, escalation, fatigue, choreography-arc

## Extraction 2 — 21-Minute Oner Breakdown
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2R_C_4Ezh2U
- Source: https://www.netflix.com/tudum/videos/extraction-2-fight-sequence-break-down
- Study: Sam Hargrave and Chris Hemsworth break down the centerpiece action sequence designed to appear as one continuous shot across prison combat, vehicles and large-scale pursuit.
- Movement design: each local fight is short and goal-driven; the sequence keeps changing the tactical problem instead of simply increasing move complexity.
- Camera / editing: camera handoffs and hidden transitions preserve forward momentum while scale expands from close-quarters fighting to moving vehicles and wider geography.
- AI-video takeaway: a strong reference for fake-oner generation. Build a 15-second clip as 3 connected modules—close fight → motivated travel/handoff → new threat—and hide the transition behind a body, wall or whip move rather than asking the model for nonstop chaos.
- Tags: oner, camera-handoff, hidden-cut, close-quarters, pursuit, action-design

## Wick Is Pain — John Wick Stunt Documentary Trailer
- Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LaEjB78QLSE

- Study: the official documentary trailer offers behind-the-scenes visibility into the physical work, repetition and stunt culture behind the John Wick series.
- Movement design: useful as a process reference rather than a single fight reference—complex screen action is built from rehearsable physical beats and performer consistency.

## Stranger Things 5 — MAC-Z Battle Behind the Scenes
- Watch: https://www.netflix.com/tudum/videos/stranger-scenes-chapter-four
- Study: the official breakdown shows a full action-production pipeline from sketches and storyboards through previs, stunt rehearsal, camera choreography and CG stand-ins.
- Movement design: multiple foreground, midground and background events are assigned separate tasks so a large creature battle remains legible instead of becoming one undifferentiated swarm.
- Camera / editing: parts of the attack are designed to feel like a documentary-style continuous move; the operator memorizes stunt beats, matches frames between pieces and uses hidden splices to preserve momentum.
- AI-video takeaway: treat the camera path as choreography. Lock eyelines and creature scale with stand-ins, assign one action to each depth layer, and hide transitions behind motivated camera or foreground movement.
- Tags: previs, stunt-vis, creature-fight, hidden-cut, camera-choreography, eyeline, 2026

## Rurouni Kenshin: The Beginning / The Final — Road To Kenshin Special Edition
- Source / official making-of hub: https://wwws.warnerbros.co.jp/rurouni-kenshin2020/news/
- Study: official production material for a live-action sword series known for committed performer movement, grounded footwork and explosive but readable sword exchanges.
- Movement design: weapon arcs, entries, evasions and body turns are rehearsed as connected physical phrases before cinematic speed and impact are layered on top.
- Camera / editing: camera position stays tied to attack direction and performer spacing, helping fast sword motion remain understandable even when the pace spikes.
- AI-video takeaway: first define attack side, sword path, parry side and recovery pose; only after those are stable should the prompt add speed ramps, cloth motion or impact effects.
- Tags: swordfight, samurai, live-action, rehearsal, weapon-arc, camera-side, stunt-choreography
